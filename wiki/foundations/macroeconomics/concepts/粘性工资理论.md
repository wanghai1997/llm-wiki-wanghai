# 粘性工资理论

The **sticky wage theory** explains why the short-run aggregate supply (SRAS) curve slopes upward: nominal wages are set by long-term contracts and cannot adjust immediately to changes in the price level. When the price level rises unexpectedly, real wages fall, making labor cheaper for firms, which increases employment and output.

## The Mechanism

### Wage Setting

Nominal wages ($W$) are typically set by contracts that last for one to three years:
- Union contracts negotiate wage levels for multi-year periods
- Implicit agreements and social norms create wage rigidity even without formal contracts
- Firms may avoid cutting nominal wages because of morale and productivity concerns (Akerlof 1982 "gift exchange")

### The Short-Run Response to Price Changes

When the overall price level ($P$) rises unexpectedly:

$$\frac{W}{P} \downarrow \Rightarrow \text{real wage falls} \Rightarrow \text{firms hire more workers} \Rightarrow Y \uparrow$$

Conversely, when $P$ falls unexpectedly:

$$\frac{W}{P} \uparrow \Rightarrow \text{real wage rises} \Rightarrow \text{firms lay off workers} \Rightarrow Y \downarrow$$

This creates the upward-sloping SRAS:

$$Y = \bar{Y} + \alpha(P - P^e)$$

where $P^e$ is the expected price level built into wage contracts.

### The Long-Run Adjustment

Over time, as contracts are renegotiated:
- Workers demand higher nominal wages to restore their real wages
- $W$ adjusts upward, returning $W/P$ to its equilibrium level
- Employment and output return to the natural rate ($\bar{Y}$)
- The economy moves back to the long-run aggregate supply (LRAS) curve

## Historical and Empirical Basis

### Supporting Evidence

- **Union contracts**: In the US, major union contracts typically last 2–3 years, creating explicit nominal rigidity
- **Downward nominal wage rigidity**: Bewley (1999) found that employers strongly resist cutting nominal wages, fearing worker morale and productivity losses
- **European unemployment**: High minimum wages and strong union contracts in continental Europe are often cited as contributing to persistent unemployment

### Challenging Evidence

- **COLA clauses**: Cost-of-living adjustment clauses in many contracts partially index wages to inflation, reducing the real wage effect (Bils 1985)
- **Non-union sectors**: In the US, only about 10% of workers are unionized; the majority have no formal wage contracts
- **Flexible fringe benefits**: Employers may adjust benefits, hours, or intensity rather than base wages
- **Europe paradox**: Countries with high union density (Nordic) often have lower unemployment than those with intermediate density (France, Italy), suggesting the relationship between wage-setting institutions and outcomes is complex

## 反面论点与数据空白

### [CONTRADICTION-1] 粘性工资的实证地位动摇

Ch 14-1将粘性工资作为SRAS三大解释之一,但实证证据复杂:
- **Bils (1985)** / **Barattieri et al. (2014)**:美国工资合同并非完全固定名义工资,大量合同含COLA条款(cost-of-living adjustment),使实际工资对未预期通胀的反应减弱
- **欧洲经验**:工会密度高的国家(北欧)工资灵活性反而更高,因为集中谈判 internalizes 外部性
- **2008后**:名义工资在美国确实向下刚性,但这与"未预期通胀降低实际工资→增雇"的机制不完全一致——衰退期是名义工资不能下降,而非通胀意外

教材对粘性工资的呈现过于简化,未讨论制度差异和合同结构的重要性。

### [CONTRADICTION-2] 向下刚性 vs 向上粘性

粘性工资理论假设工资"不能调整",但实际上:
- **向下刚性**（不能降）有强实证支持（Bewley 1999）
- **向上粘性**（不能迅速涨）的证据较弱
- 2008危机后,美国失业率高企但通胀未大幅下降,与工资向下刚性一致;但2021-2022年工资迅速上涨,显示向上调整可以很快

这意味着"粘性"是**不对称**的——教材的 symmetric 处理可能误导。

### [CONTRADICTION-3] 微观基础不足

教材将粘性工资作为"假设",但为什么不建微基础?
- **效率工资**:企业主动支付高于市场出清的工资以提高生产力（Shapiro-Stiglitz 1984）
- **隐性合同**:企业为风险厌恶的工人提供工资保险（Azariadis 1975, Baily 1974）
- **搜索与匹配**:工资由议价决定,而非市场出清（Diamond-Mortensen-Pissarides）

这些模型解释了为什么工资不立即调整,但教材在Ch 14只简要提及,未深入。

### 数据空白

1. **工资调整的频率和幅度**:Bils-Klenow-Malin (2018) 发现美国工资调整平均间隔约12-18个月,但幅度较大
2. **零工经济**:Uber/外卖骑手无固定工资合同,粘性工资理论对其不适用
3. **中国劳动力市场**:户籍制度、 migrant worker 制度使工资决定机制与西方差异巨大
