# 不完全信息理论

The **imperfect information theory** of aggregate supply, developed by [[Robert Lucas Jr]] (1972), explains why the short-run aggregate supply (SRAS) curve slopes upward without assuming any nominal rigidities (sticky wages or prices). Instead, it relies on **signal extraction problems**: producers confuse changes in the overall price level with changes in their own product's relative price.

## The Core Idea

When producers observe a change in the nominal price of their product, they face a fundamental uncertainty:
- Is the price change due to a shift in **aggregate demand** (affecting all goods)?
- Or is it due to a shift in **relative demand** for their specific product?

If producers could perfectly distinguish between these two types of shocks, they would never change output in response to aggregate demand shocks (consistent with classical neutrality). But with imperfect information, they must make educated guesses.

### The Signal Extraction Problem

Suppose a producer observes an increase in the nominal price of their product ($p_i$):

$$p_i = p + (p_i - p)$$

where:
- $p$ = aggregate price level (common to all goods)
- $(p_i - p)$ = relative price of good $i$

The producer cannot directly observe $p$ or $(p_i - p)$ separately. They must infer these from noisy signals. Lucas assumed that producers know the **distribution** of shocks but not their current realizations.

### The Result

Lucas showed that:
- When the observed price increase is **small**, producers attribute a larger fraction of it to relative demand (because relative shocks are typically smaller than aggregate shocks)
- They therefore **increase production**
- This creates an upward-sloping SRAS: higher aggregate demand raises the price level, which producers partially misinterpret as favorable relative price shifts, increasing output

### The Lucas Supply Function

The formal result is the **Lucas supply function**:

$$y = \bar{y} + \gamma(P - P^e)$$

where $P^e$ is the expected price level. This looks identical to the sticky-price SRAS, but the mechanism is entirely different: **no nominal rigidities**, only information imperfections.

## Key Implications

### The Phillips Curve

Lucas's model provides a **microfounded Phillips curve**:
- Unanticipated monetary expansion raises $P$ above $P^e$
- Output rises above potential
- As producers learn the truth (information improves), $P^e$ adjusts and output returns to $\bar{y}$
- **Systematic monetary policy has no real effects** (policy ineffectiveness proposition)

### The Role of Policy Predictability

The Lucas model implies that **only unanticipated policy matters**:
- If the central bank follows a predictable rule, producers fully anticipate the price level changes
- $P = P^e$, so output remains at $\bar{y}$
- Only "surprise" deviations from the rule affect real variables

This was the theoretical foundation for the rational expectations critique of Keynesian demand management.

## 反面论点与数据空白

### [CONTRADICTION-1] 信息时滞假设不现实

Lucas模型假设生产者需要**一段时间**才能获知总体价格水平,但现实中:
- **CPI发布**:美国CPI每月发布,滞后约2-3周
- **实时数据**:现代信息技术和金融新闻使 aggregate data 几乎即时可得
- **专业预测**:Blue Chip、Survey of Professional Forecasters 等提供广泛可获得的一致性预期
- **互联网**:Google Trends、实时零售数据使价格信息前所未有地可及

如果信息摩擦如此之小,不完全信息理论难以解释显著且持续的产出波动。

### [CONTRADICTION-2] 未解释2008后的持续失业

Lucas模型预测产出很快回到潜在水平(一旦信息更新),但:
- **2008后**:美国失业率在5年以上远高于自然率
- **欧元区**:南部国家持续高失业超过10年
- **日本**:"失去的二十年"

这些持久偏离不能用"信息时滞"解释,需要粘性价格、金融摩擦或滞后效应等其他机制。

### [CONTRADICTION-3] 与微观证据的矛盾

微观研究显示企业**确实**知道总体经济状况:
- **企业调查**:NFIB、Duke CFO Survey 显示企业高度关注宏观经济指标
- **行业数据**:企业密切跟踪行业价格指数和竞争对手定价
- **供应链**:B2B 定价高度透明,企业清楚知道成本变化来自投入品还是整体通胀

这与Lucas假设的"混淆总体与相对价格"不一致。

### 数据空白

1. **信息摩擦的量化**:衡量 aggregate information vs 相对信息在企业决策中的权重困难
2. **异质性信息**:不同行业、不同规模企业的信息获取能力差异巨大
3. **行为维度**:即使信息可得,企业是否理性处理?行为偏见可能使"信息充足但判断错误"
4. **大数据时代**:实时高频数据(卫星、信用卡、移动设备)正在消灭传统信息摩擦
