# 促进增长的政策

What policies can governments adopt to promote economic growth? Mankiw Ch 9-3 evaluates several categories of growth-promoting policies, drawing on the [[Solow增长模型]] and its extensions. The analysis centers on whether the US (and by implication, other developed economies) is below the [[黄金律资本水平]], and what levers policymakers have to raise long-run living standards.

## Policy Categories

### 1. Saving and Investment

The Solow model predicts that higher saving rates raise steady-state per-capita income. Policies to encourage saving:
- **Tax-advantaged savings accounts** (IRAs, 401(k) plans)
- **Public pension reform** (reducing pay-as-you-go systems that crowd out private saving)
- **Budget surpluses** (public saving complements private saving)
- **Financial development** (reducing borrowing constraints so firms can fund productive investment)

**Evaluation**: The US appears to be **below the Golden Rule** ($MPK - \delta \approx 8\% > n + g \approx 3\%$), suggesting more saving could raise long-run consumption. However:
- Higher saving means lower consumption today
- The transition takes decades
- If the economy were above the Golden Rule, more saving would reduce welfare (dynamic inefficiency)

### 2. Investment Allocation

Not all investment is equally productive. Policies to improve investment quality:
- **Reducing tax distortions** that favor housing over business capital
- **Eliminating subsidies** to declining industries
- **Promoting venture capital** and equity financing (rather than bank debt)
- **Infrastructure investment** with high social returns

### 3. Education and Human Capital

The [[Mankiw-Romer-Weil模型]] shows human capital is a major determinant of steady-state income:
- **School quality** (not just quantity) matters for productivity
- **Vocational training** and apprenticeships
- **Higher education** subsidies (with attention to ROI)
- **Adult retraining** programs for technological displacement

### 4. Free Trade and International Integration

Mankiw's textbook strongly endorses free trade as growth-promoting:
- **Technology diffusion**: Trade exposes domestic firms to foreign best practices
- **Scale effects**: Access to larger markets raises returns to innovation
- **Competition**: Foreign competition forces domestic firms to improve efficiency
- **Resource allocation**: Trade allows countries to specialize according to comparative advantage

**Empirical basis**: Frankel-Romer (1999) use geographic instruments to identify trade's causal effect on income, finding a strong positive relationship.

### 5. Research and Development

Since long-run growth depends on technological progress (see [[内生增长理论]]), policies to promote innovation:
- **R&D tax credits** and direct subsidies
- **Patent protection** (balancing incentives against access costs)
- **University research funding**
- **Immigration of skilled workers** (especially scientists and engineers)

### 6. Institutions

Perhaps the most important but least tractable policy lever (see [[制度与经济增长]]):
- **Property rights** and contract enforcement
- **Rule of law** and judicial independence
- **Corruption control**
- **Political stability**

## [CONTRADICTION-4] Free Trade: "Overwhelming Evidence"?

Mankiw Ch 9-3 Case Study ("Is Free Trade Good for Economic Growth?", p.259-260) concludes: "The overwhelming weight of the evidence...is that Adam Smith was right." However:

1. **Correlation ≠ causation**: While Frankel-Romer use IV strategies, Rodrik and others have critiqued their model specification. Geography may affect income through channels other than trade (disease, institutions).

2. **Distributional effects**: The textbook treats growth as uniformly beneficial, but trade creates winners and losers within countries. The "China shock" (Autor-Dorn-Hanson 2013) demonstrated that import competition can devastate specific regions and industries.

3. **Missing the 2010s deglobalization**: The 2016 textbook predates the full force of:
   - Trump tariffs (2018–present)
   - Brexit (2020)
   - COVID-19 supply chain disruptions
   - "Friend-shoring" and industrial policy revival
   - National security concerns over semiconductor and critical mineral supply chains

The textbook's confident "overwhelming evidence" framing ignores these challenges to the free trade consensus—a significant [regional-bias] reflecting its American perspective during the pre-2016 globalization era.

## [BIAS-2] 产业政策的"美国案例霸权"

Ch 9-3 的产业政策讨论几乎全部来自美国案例：
- Alexander Hamilton 的关税保护
- ARPANET/互联网（政府资助创新的成功案例）
- Solyndra 失败（政府选错赢家的警示 tale）
- Walmart 零售创新（私人部门效率）

日本 MITI/Honda 是唯一非美正面案例，且以 "MITI 失败、Honda 自主成功" 的叙事呈现。以下案例完全缺席：
- 韩国财阀+政府导向的产业政策（钢铁、造船、半导体）
- 台湾的半导体产业培育（工研院、联电、台积电）
- 新加坡的政府投资公司和主权财富基金
- 中国的高铁网络、光伏产业、电动车产业政策

这种选择性呈现强化了"政府失灵"的预设偏见，忽略了东亚发展型国家的成功经验。

## 反面论点与数据空白

### [BIAS] "增长是好的"预设
教材隐含假设更高的 GDP 增长总是 desirable。但：
- **环境约束**：无限增长在有限星球上的可行性（"去增长"运动、Doughnut Economics）
- **福祉测度**：Stiglitz-Sen-Fitoussi (2009) 指出 GDP 未能捕捉福祉的多维性
- **不平等**：增长收益可能被顶层人群攫取，中位数工人未必受益
- **工作时间**：更高的 GDP 可能伴随更长的工作时间和更少 leisure

### 数据空白
- **气候政策与增长**：Stern Review (2006) 估计气候变化的福利成本，提出碳预算约束下的最优增长路径。教材完全未讨论增长与环境的 tradeoff
- **AI 与劳动力市场**：AI 自动化可能提高 TFP，但也可能引发大规模技术性失业。标准的增长模型假设充分就业，无法分析这一 tension
- **不平等与增长**：Piketty (2014) 的 r > g 假说暗示 capitalism 天然趋向财富集中，可能需要累进税制和财富税来维持增长的社会基础。教材的政策讨论完全忽略分配维度
- **中国模式**：中国的增长经验（高储蓄+高投资+产业政策+人口红利）部分符合 Solow 框架，但其制度因素（地方政府竞争、土地财政、国企角色）完全超出教材范围
