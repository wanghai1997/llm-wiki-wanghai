# 货币需求理论

**Money demand theory** explains why economic agents hold money—a non-interest-bearing asset—when alternative assets (bonds, stocks) yield positive returns. Mankiw 9e Ch 18 traces the evolution from the simple quantity theory to sophisticated portfolio and transaction models, each capturing a different motive for holding money.

## Three Motives for Holding Money

| Motive | Driver | Key Model |
|---|---|---|
| **Transactions** | Income-expenditure timing mismatch | Quantity theory, [[Baumol-Tobin模型]] |
| **Precautionary** | Uncertainty about future cash needs | Miller-Orr model (beyond textbook) |
| **Speculative** | Expected returns on alternative assets | Keynes (1936), [[货币需求投资组合理论]] |

## From Quantity Theory to Asset Demand

### 1. Quantity Theory: $MV = PY$

The simplest form assumes velocity $V$ is constant:

$$(M/P)^d = kY$$

- Money demand proportional to income
- No role for interest rates
- Useful for long-run relationships, fails for short-run dynamics

### 2. Interest-Sensitive Demand: $(M/P)^d = L(i, Y)$

Money demand depends negatively on the nominal interest rate (opportunity cost of holding money):

$$L(i, Y) = Y \cdot e^{-\eta i}$$

This is the foundation of the LM curve in IS-LM analysis.

### 3. Inventory-Theoretic Model: Baumol-Tobin

Households optimize between holding money (convenient, no interest) and bonds (interest-earning, conversion cost):

$$M^* = \sqrt{\frac{bY}{2i}}$$

See [[Baumol-Tobin模型]]

### 4. Portfolio Theory: Tobin (1958)

Money is one asset in a mean-variance portfolio. Its demand depends on its risk-return characteristics relative to bonds and stocks.

See [[货币需求投资组合理论]]

## The Stability of Money Demand

A stable money demand function is essential for monetary policy:
- **1960s–1970s**: Goldfeld (1973) found stable US money demand
- **1980s**: Goldfeld (1976) "The Case of the Missing Money"—money demand shifted unpredictably
- **Post-2008**: Massive monetary expansion (QE) with minimal inflation—$L(\cdot)$ appeared to change structurally

See [[货币需求的经验证据]]

## 反面论点与数据空白

- **数字支付的冲击**: 信用卡、移动支付、数字货币使"持有货币"的概念模糊化。M2的定义在电子支付时代失去了清晰的经济含义。
- **负利率的悖论**: 当 $i < 0$ 时，持有现金(零利率)优于存款(负利率)。但现金的存储成本和安全性问题使"货币需求无限大"的极端预测不成立。
- **加密货币的分流**: 稳定币、CBDC可能替代传统货币的部分功能，但监管不确定性和波动性使它们尚未成为主流交易媒介。
- **regional-bias**: 货币需求研究以美国为主。发展中国家(高通胀、美元化、非正规金融)中货币需求函数的形式和稳定性可能根本不同。
