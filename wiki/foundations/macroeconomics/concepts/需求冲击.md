# 需求冲击

A **demand shock** is an event that shifts the [[总需求曲线]], causing output and the price level to deviate from their expected paths. Demand shocks are the primary source of short-run economic fluctuations in the Keynesian framework.

## Sources of Demand Shocks

Demand shocks originate from changes in any component of aggregate expenditure:

### Consumption Shocks ($\Delta C$)
- **Wealth effects**: Stock market or housing booms/busts change household net worth
- **Consumer confidence**: Pessimism leads to precautionary saving (Paradox of Thrift)
- **Tax policy**: Changes in disposable income

### Investment Shocks ($\Delta I$)
- **Animal spirits**: Keynes's term for irrational waves of optimism/pessimism
- **Interest rate changes**: Central bank policy or credit market conditions
- **Credit crunches**: Banking crises restrict firm borrowing (financial accelerator)
- **Technology and regulation**: Changes in expected profitability

### Government Purchases Shocks ($\Delta G$)
- Fiscal stimulus or austerity
- War spending
- Infrastructure investment

### Net Export Shocks ($\Delta NX$)
- Foreign recessions or booms
- Exchange rate fluctuations
- Trade policy (tariffs, quotas)

## Effects in the AD-AS Framework

### Positive Demand Shock (AD shifts right)

**Short run**: Output rises above potential ($Y > \bar{Y}$), price level rises.
- Labor market tightens, unemployment falls below natural rate
- Firms increase production, profits rise

**Long-run adjustment**: Wages and input prices gradually rise → SRAS shifts left → economy returns to $\bar{Y}$ at a higher price level.

### Negative Demand Shock (AD shifts left)

**Short run**: Output falls below potential ($Y < \bar{Y}$), price level falls (or inflation slows).
- Unemployment rises above natural rate
- Excess capacity, idle capital

**Long-run adjustment**: Wages and input prices gradually fall → SRAS shifts right → economy returns to $\bar{Y}$.

## Historical Examples

### The Great Depression (1929–1933)
The most severe negative demand shock in modern history:
- Stock market crash destroyed wealth ($\Delta C < 0$)
- Banking panics caused credit freeze ($\Delta I < 0$)
- Gold standard constrained monetary response
- US real GDP fell 30%; unemployment peaked at 25%

### The 2008 Financial Crisis
- Housing bubble burst destroyed household wealth
- Financial institutions failed, credit markets froze
- Global trade collapsed
- Governments enacted unprecedented fiscal/monetary stimulus

### The 1990s US Boom
- IT investment surge (dot-com boom)
- Rising stock market wealth
- Fiscal consolidation + monetary easing
- $Y$ persistently above $\bar{Y}$ with low inflation (productivity miracle)

## 反面论点与数据空白

### [CONTRADICTION-1] 需求冲击 vs 供给冲击:难以区分

教材将需求冲击和供给冲击作为互斥类别,但现实中往往同时发生:
- **2008危机**:银行危机(金融供给冲击)→信贷紧缩→AD下降(需求冲击)→企业破产→长期产能损失(供给冲击)
- **2020疫情**:封锁→供给中断 + 恐慌性储蓄→需求崩溃
- **俄乌战争(2022)**:能源制裁→供给冲击 + 信心崩溃→需求冲击

将冲击简单归类为"需求"或"供给"可能误导政策选择。

### [CONTRADICTION-2] 财富效应的实证微弱性

教材强调财富效应($P_{asset} \uparrow \Rightarrow C \uparrow$),但实证证据:
- **住房财富效应** (Case-Shiller):每增加1美元房产净值,消费增加约0.03-0.09美元
- **股票财富效应**:更微弱,且主要集中在富裕群体(边际消费倾向低)
- **中国**:房价上涨并未带来相应的消费繁荣,反而因高储蓄和房贷挤压抑制消费

### [CONTRADICTION-3] 动物精神的不可操作性

Keynes的"animal spirits"被教材引用以解释投资波动,但:
- 无法被直接测度或预测
- 现代行为宏观经济学(Angeletos-La'O 2013)试图用"sentiment shocks"形式化,但仍处于前沿
- 将重大经济事件归因于"不可解释的心理波动"可能掩盖结构性因素(制度、金融结构、全球失衡)

### 数据空白

1. **实时冲击识别**:NBER在衰退开始后平均需要8个月才确认拐点——政策制定者在行动时无法确定冲击类型
2. **乘数的异质性**:财政乘数在金融危机期间约为1.5-2.0,但在正常时期可能接近0——教材使用单一乘数
3. **全球溢出**:一国需求 shock 通过贸易和金融渠道传导至全球,但教材以封闭或小型开放经济为主
