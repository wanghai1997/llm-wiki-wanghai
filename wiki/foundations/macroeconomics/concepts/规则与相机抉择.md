# 规则与相机抉择

The **rules vs. discretion debate** is one of the oldest and most consequential disputes in macroeconomic policy. Should central banks and governments follow fixed, pre-announced policy rules, or should they retain flexibility to respond to unexpected circumstances? Mankiw 9e Ch 19-3 frames this as a tension between predictability and flexibility.

## The Case for Rules

### Friedman (1960): The K-Percent Rule

[[Milton Friedman]] proposed that the money supply should grow at a constant rate (e.g., 3-5% annually), regardless of economic conditions:
- Eliminates human error and political bias
- Provides a nominal anchor for expectations
- Prevents the "stop-go" cycles of activist policy

### Kydland-Prescott (1977): Time Inconsistency

Discretionary policy creates an **inflation bias**: policymakers announce low inflation to anchor expectations, but once expectations are set, they have an incentive to surprise with expansionary policy.

Result: **higher inflation with no output gain**.

See [[时间不一致性]]

### Taylor Rule as a Compromise

[[John Taylor]] (1993) proposed a **simple, transparent rule**:

$$i = r^* + \pi + 0.5(\pi - \pi^*) + 0.5(y - y^*)$$

- Responds systematically to inflation and output gaps
- Provides accountability and predictability
- Retains some flexibility (parameters can be adjusted)

## The Case for Discretion

### Keynesian Argument
- Economic shocks are heterogeneous and unpredictable
- A rigid rule cannot respond to novel crises (2008, 2020)
- Financial innovation and structural change alter transmission mechanisms

### The "Constrained Discretion" Approach

Modern central banks practice **constrained discretion**:
- **Target**: Explicit inflation target (e.g., 2%)
- **Strategy**: Flexible approach to achieving target
- **Communication**: Forward guidance shapes expectations
- **Accountability**: Must explain deviations from target

This combines the credibility benefits of rules with the flexibility of discretion.

## Empirical Evidence

### Rules Perform Better
- Countries with explicit inflation targets have lower and more stable inflation (Mishkin-Schmidt-Hebbel 2007)
- Independent central banks ( insulated from political pressure) achieve better outcomes

### Discretion Saved the Day
- 2008: Unconventional policies (QE, forward guidance) were not in any rule
- 2020: Massive fiscal/monetary coordination required ad hoc responses
- Rules cannot handle zero lower bound or financial crisis scenarios

## 反面论点与数据空白

- **最优规则的不可知**: Taylor规则的系数(0.5, 0.5)来自对美国1980s-1990s数据的拟合，但最优系数可能随经济结构变化而不同。Orphanides (2003) 证明：使用实时数据（而非修正后数据）估计的最优规则系数差异很大。
- **规则本身也可能时间不一致**: 如果危机需要偏离规则，规则承诺的可信性可能受损。完全承诺不可行——未来不确定性太大。
- **多重目标冲突**: 现代央行面临多目标（价格稳定、充分就业、金融稳定、气候风险）。单一规则无法同时优化所有目标。
- **regional-bias**: 规则辩论以美联储为中心。欧央行双支柱、日本央行QQE、中国央行多目标制的框架与"规则vs相机"二分不完全对应。
- **regional-bias - 中国"规则 + 相机"的混合体**:[[孙国峰]] 《健全现代货币政策框架》(2021) 显示中国不采用纯 Taylor 规则,而是**"规则 + 相机"混合体**——

  - **规则元素**:[[LPR贷款市场报价利率|LPR 形成机制]](MLF + 加点)的部分规则化、[[M2与社会融资规模|M2 与名义 GDP 基本匹配]] 的总量锚、[[跨周期政策设计]] 的多周期协调。
  - **相机抉择元素**:[[结构性货币政策工具]] 按经济发展不同时期动态调整支持重点;政策利率调整非机械响应通胀 / 产出缺口;基于"国情"的判断空间大。
  - **理论新形式**:孙国峰提出"**激励兼容机制**"——通过工具设计而非简单利率调整影响银行行为,实质是"在规则与相机间增加微观基础调控层"。

  Taylor 规则在中国的实证检验:Burdekin-Siklos 2008、He-Pauwels 2008 等研究显示中国 7 天回购利率与 Taylor 规则推导值的拟合度有限;PBoC 决策受多重因素(增长、就业、汇率、金融稳定)综合影响,**单一规则不能解释 PBoC 行为**。详见 [[现代货币政策框架]] / [[央行三大约束-流动性资本利率]]。
