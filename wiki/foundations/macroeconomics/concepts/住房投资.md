# 住房投资

**Residential investment** is the construction of new housing units, a component of GDP investment that combines features of both durable consumption and capital investment. Housing is simultaneously a consumer good (providing shelter services) and an investment asset (whose price fluctuates with market conditions). Mankiw 9e Ch 17-3 analyzes housing through the lens of stock-flow dynamics.

## Housing as a Stock-Flow Problem

The housing market has two layers:

1. **Stock**: The existing housing stock $H$ provides housing services
2. **Flow**: New construction $I_H$ adds to the stock; depreciation $\delta H$ reduces it

$$\Delta H = I_H - \delta H$$

## The User Cost of Housing

A homeowner implicitly "rents" housing from themselves. The implicit rental cost (user cost) is:

$$\text{User Cost} = (r + \delta)P_H - \dot{P}_H^e$$

where $P_H$ is the house price, $r$ is the real interest rate, $\delta$ is maintenance/depreciation, and $\dot{P}_H^e$ is expected capital gains.

**Key implication**: Housing investment is highly sensitive to real interest rates (through $r$) and to expected price appreciation.

## The Housing Boom and Bust (2000s)

### The Boom (2002–2006)
- Low interest rates (Fed funds rate 1% in 2003)
- Loose lending standards (subprime mortgages, NINJA loans)
- Expectations of ever-rising prices
- Housing investment/GDP rose from ~4.5% to ~6.2%

### The Bust (2007–2012)
- Interest rate reset on adjustable-rate mortgages
- Subprime defaults triggered contagion
- House prices fell 30%+ nationally
- Housing investment/GDP collapsed to ~2.5%

The housing collapse was both a **cause** and **consequence** of the 2008 financial crisis.

## Housing and the Business Cycle

Housing investment is among the most volatile components of GDP:
- **Interest sensitivity**: Mortgage rates directly affect affordability
- **Wealth effects**: Rising house prices increase household net worth → consumption
- **Collateral channel**: Home equity backs consumer borrowing
- **Regional variation**: Housing cycles differ dramatically across cities/states

## 反面论点与数据空白

- **需求侧 vs 供给侧**: 教材侧重需求（利率、收入），但住房投资受供给侧约束（分区法、建筑许可、劳动力短缺）同样严重。美国主要城市的住房短缺主要是规制问题，而非需求不足。
- **金融化扭曲**: 2000s住房泡沫中，投资银行、对冲基金、CDO市场的参与使住房从"居住消费"变为"投机资产"——传统住房投资模型不处理金融化。
- **代际分配**: 房价上涨使有房者受益、无房者受损——住房投资周期具有强烈的分配后果，标准模型不处理。
- **国际比较**: 德国（租房为主、房价稳定）、日本（泡沫后长期停滞）、中国（预售制、地方政府土地财政）——住房投资制度差异巨大，教材以美国为模板。
- **气候风险**: 洪水区、野火区的住房投资正在积累"搁浅资产"——传统模型假设地理风险不变。
