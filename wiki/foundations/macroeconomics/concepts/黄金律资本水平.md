# 黄金律资本水平

The **Golden Rule level of capital** is the steady-state capital stock $k^*_{gold}$ that **maximizes consumption per capita** (or per effective worker, with technological progress). It was derived by [[Edmund Phelps]] in his 1961 paper *"The Golden Rule of Accumulation"* (American Economic Review).

## Derivation

In the steady state of the Solow model, all output is divided between consumption and replacement investment:

$$c^* = f(k^*) - \delta k^*$$

To maximize $c^*$ with respect to $k^*$, take the derivative and set to zero:

$$\frac{dc^*}{dk^*} = f'(k^*) - \delta = 0$$

Thus the **Golden Rule condition** (basic model, no population growth or technology):

$$MPK = \delta$$

### With Population Growth and Technological Progress

When population grows at rate $n$ and technology at rate $g$, break-even investment is $(\delta + n + g)\tilde{k}$. Consumption per effective worker is:

$$\tilde{c}^* = f(\tilde{k}^*) - (\delta + n + g)\tilde{k}^*$$

Maximizing yields:

$$MPK = \delta + n + g$$

Or equivalently:

$$MPK - \delta = n + g$$

The net marginal product of capital must equal the economy's growth rate.

## Dynamic Efficiency vs Inefficiency

Comparing the actual steady state to the Golden Rule:

| Condition | Interpretation | Policy |
|---|---|---|
| $MPK - \delta > n + g$ | **Below Golden Rule** (capital scarce) | Higher saving increases long-run consumption |
| $MPK - \delta = n + g$ | **At Golden Rule** (optimal) | Current saving rate is optimal |
| $MPK - \delta < n + g$ | **Above Golden Rule** (dynamic inefficiency) | Lower saving increases long-run consumption |

### Dynamic Inefficiency

When $k^* > k^*_{gold}$ (above Golden Rule), the economy has **overaccumulated capital**. Reducing $s$ would:
- Reduce $k^*$ toward $k^*_{gold}$
- Raise consumption both during transition and in the new steady state
- Make every generation better off

This is called **dynamic inefficiency** because the economy is saving too much—sacrificing present consumption for future consumption in a way that reduces welfare at all points in time.

The [[Diamond 重叠世代模型]] (1965) is the standard framework for analyzing dynamic inefficiency; it can arise in market economies with imperfect intergenerational transfers. Mankiw's textbook does not cover the OLG model.

## Empirical Application: Is the US at the Golden Rule?

Mankiw (Ch 9-3) presents the following estimates for the US:
- Capital's share of income: $\alpha \approx 1/3$
- Capital-output ratio: $K/Y \approx 2.5$
- Therefore: $MPK = \alpha Y/K \approx (1/3)(1/2.5) = 0.133$ or **13.3%**
- Depreciation: $\delta \approx 4\%$
- Growth rate: $n + g \approx 3\%$

So: $MPK - \delta \approx 9.3\% > 3\% = n + g$

The US appears to be **well below the Golden Rule**, suggesting that higher saving could increase long-run consumption.

**Caveats**:
- The MPK calculation assumes a Cobb-Douglas production function
- Capital share may be higher than 1/3 when including human capital and intangibles
- The marginal product of installed capital may differ from the average return
- The Ramsey criterion ($MPK - \delta = \rho + \theta g$, where $\rho$ is time preference) may differ from the Golden Rule

## The "Golden Rule" Name

Phelps named the condition by analogy to the biblical Golden Rule ("do unto others as you would have them do unto you"). In the Golden Rule steady state, each generation consumes exactly what it would want previous generations to have consumed—no generation is asked to sacrifice for the benefit of others. This is also the steady state that a **social planner with zero discount rate** would choose.

## 反面论点与数据空白

### [CONTRADICTION] Golden Rule vs Ramsey Optimum
The textbook presents the Golden Rule as the welfare-maximizing steady state, but this is only true under restrictive assumptions. In the Ramsey-Cass-Koopmans model (omitted from the textbook), the optimal steady state satisfies:

$$MPK - \delta = \rho + \theta g$$

where $\rho$ is the rate of time preference and $\theta$ is the inverse elasticity of intertemporal substitution. Since $\rho > 0$ (people discount the future), the Ramsey-optimal capital stock is **below** the Golden Rule level. The textbook's implicit identification of "Golden Rule" with "optimal" is misleading. A society that actually reached the Golden Rule would be over-saving from the perspective of its present members.

### [BIAS] Physical Capital Narrowness
The textbook's MPK calculation focuses on physical capital only. Modern economies' "capital" includes:
- **Human capital** (education, skills): Measured returns to schooling are 8-10% per year
- **Intangible capital** (R&D, software, brand equity): Now exceeds physical capital in value for many firms
- **Natural capital** (resources, ecosystem services): Often depleted rather than accumulated

A comprehensive assessment of whether the US "saves enough" would need to incorporate all forms of capital. The textbook's narrow focus on physical capital may understate the true social return to investment or overstate the gap below the Golden Rule.

### 数据空白
- **OLG 模型**: Diamond (1965) 的世代交叠模型是分析动态无效性的标准工具，教材完全未出现
- **Piketty 的 r > g**: Piketty (2014) 强调资本回报率 r 长期大于经济增长率 g，这与 Solow 框架下 Golden Rule 要求 $r = g$ 形成张力。若 $r > g$ 是常态，资本主义经济系统性地低于 Golden Rule，需要解释为何市场未能自动调整
- **气候约束下的资本积累**：标准 Golden Rule 不考虑碳预算或自然资本折旧。在气候转型背景下，"最优资本水平"必须纳入环境约束
