# 收敛假说

The **convergence hypothesis** asks whether poor economies tend to grow faster than rich ones, thereby "catching up" over time. It is one of the most tested empirical predictions of the [[Solow增长模型]] and has generated one of the most important debates in growth economics.

## Types of Convergence

### Absolute (Unconditional) Convergence

Poor countries grow faster than rich countries **regardless of their characteristics**.

The basic Solow model predicts absolute convergence: countries with lower initial $k$ (below their common steady state $k^*$) experience faster growth as they accumulate capital. The transition equation near the steady state implies:

$$\frac{\dot{y}}{y} \approx \lambda (\ln y^* - \ln y)$$

where $\lambda$ is the convergence speed (~2% per year for typical parameters).

**Empirical test**: Regress growth rate on initial income:
$$g_i = \alpha + \beta \ln(y_{i0}) + \epsilon_i$$

Absolute convergence requires $\beta < 0$.

**Result**: **Rejected** for the full cross-country sample. There is no tendency for poor countries to grow faster unconditionally.

### Conditional Convergence

Countries converge to **their own steady states**, which differ based on structural characteristics. A country grows faster when it is further below its own steady state.

**Empirical test**: Augment the regression with controls for determinants of steady state ($s$, $n$, human capital, institutions):
$$g_i = \alpha + \beta \ln(y_{i0}) + \gamma_1 s_i + \gamma_2 n_i + \gamma_3 h_i + \epsilon_i$$

**Result**: **Supported**. $\beta < 0$ and statistically significant when conditioning on steady-state determinants. The estimated convergence speed is roughly **2% per year**.

Interpretation: Countries converge to their own steady states at about 2% per year. A country halfway to its steady state closes half the gap in ~35 years.

### $\sigma$-Convergence vs $\beta$-Convergence

- **$\beta$-convergence**: Poor countries grow faster (negative relationship between initial income and growth rate)
- **$\sigma$-convergence**: The cross-sectional dispersion of income declines over time

$\beta$-convergence is necessary but not sufficient for $\sigma$-convergence. If steady states are very different, countries can $\beta$-converge to their own paths while cross-country dispersion increases.

## Evidence

### US States (Absolute Convergence)

Baumol (1986) and Barro-Sala-i-Martin (1991, 1992) found strong absolute convergence among US states:
- Convergence speed ~2% per year
- Poor states (e.g., Mississippi in 1880) systematically grew faster than rich states (Massachusetts)
- Interpretation: US states share similar institutions, policies, and technology access, so they converge to a common steady state

### OECD Countries (Conditional Convergence)

Among rich OECD countries, conditional convergence holds:
- Countries that were below their steady states after WWII (Japan, Germany) grew rapidly
- Countries at or near steady state (US, UK) grew more slowly

### Global Cross-Section (No Absolute Convergence)

Across all countries (Penn World Tables, Maddison Project):
- No evidence of absolute convergence 1960–present
- Some poor countries grew rapidly (South Korea, China, Botswana)
- Many poor countries stagnated or declined (Zimbabwe, Venezuela, many Sub-Saharan African nations)
- The global income distribution has **widened** in absolute terms

## Why No Global Absolute Convergence?

The Solow model's prediction of absolute convergence fails globally because countries differ in:

1. **Saving/investment rates**: East Asian tigers saved 30–40% of GDP; many African nations saved <10%
2. **Population growth**: High fertility reduces capital deepening
3. **Human capital**: Education levels vary enormously
4. **Institutions**: Property rights, rule of law, corruption
5. **Technology access**: Trade openness, FDI, education
6. **Geography**: Landlockedness, tropical disease, resource endowments

The [[Mankiw-Romer-Weil模型]] (1992) showed that adding human capital to the Solow framework explains roughly 80% of cross-country income variation and restores conditional convergence.

## 反面论点与数据空白

### [CONTRADICTION] 教材对"收敛"的模糊处理
教材 Ch 9-2 先说 "there is no evidence that countries with lower initial incomes tend to grow faster"（绝对收敛被拒绝），但随后又说 "when we control for the determinants of the steady state...the data support the hypothesis of conditional convergence"。这种表述可能让学生误以为"条件收敛"是 Solow 模型的原初预测，而实际上模型原初预测的是绝对收敛。条件收敛是模型的**修正版**（加入异质性 steady states）的预测，不是基本模型的预测。

### [regional-bias] 美国各州经验的过度推广
教材以美国各州的绝对收敛（~2%/年）作为收敛假说的"教科书证据"。但：
- 美国各州共享同一货币、法律体系、语言、政治体制和资本市场
- 这种"收敛俱乐部"条件在全球几乎不存在
- 用美国各州推断全球收敛是一种严重的过度推广

### [BIAS] 收敛速度"2%定律"
教材将 ~2% 的收敛速度呈现为普遍规律。但：
- 估计对模型设定敏感（人力资本测度、样本选择）
- 对最穷国家，收敛可能更慢（贫困陷阱、制度瓶颈）
- 对快速工业化的经济体（韩国、中国），收敛速度可能更快（"后发优势"）

### 数据空白
- **俱乐部收敛**：Durlauf-Johnson (1995) 发现收敛只发生在"俱乐部"内部（相似制度/地理的国家组），而非全球范围。教材未涉及
- **多峰分布**：Quah (1993, 1997) 发现全球收入分布正在从单峰向双峰演变（"双峰化"），即富裕国家和贫穷国家各自聚集，中间层消失。这与任何形式的收敛假说矛盾
- **中国经验的特殊性**：中国的快速增长（1978–2019 年均 ~9%）既不符合绝对收敛（中国并非最穷国家），也不完全符合条件收敛（中国的制度特征难以用标准变量捕捉）。教材对中国增长模式的讨论严重不足
