# 管理实践与生产率

**Management practices**—how firms organize production, set targets, monitor performance, and incentivize workers—are a significant but historically overlooked determinant of productivity differences across firms and countries. The **World Management Survey (WMS)**, developed by [[Nicholas Bloom]] and [[John Van Reenen]], provided the first large-scale systematic evidence that management quality explains a substantial portion of the TFP differences documented in growth accounting.

## The World Management Survey

Bloom and Van Reenen developed a standardized, double-blind interview protocol to score management practices along four dimensions, each rated 1 (worst) to 5 (best):

### 1. Operations

- Use of lean manufacturing principles (just-in-time, continuous improvement/kaizen)
- Adoption of modern quality management techniques
- Documentation and standardization of processes
- Data-driven decision-making on the shop floor

### 2. Monitoring

- Regular collection and review of performance indicators
- Frequency and quality of performance dialogues between managers and workers
- Use of KPIs to track and improve operations
- Timely feedback loops

### 3. Target Setting

- Appropriateness of targets (challenging but achievable)
- Interconnection of targets across levels and functions
- Time horizon balance (short-term vs long-term goals)
- Flexibility to adjust targets based on changing conditions

### 4. Incentives/Management of People

- Linking pay to performance (individual and team)
- Promotion based on merit vs tenure/seniority
- Dealing with underperformers (retraining, reassignment, or termination)
- Retention and reward of high performers

## Key Findings

### Management Varies Enormously

Across over 10,000 firms in 34 countries:
- The gap between the 10th and 90th percentile management scores within a country is as large as the gap between average US and average developing-country firms
- **US management is highest** among early surveyed countries (US, UK, France, Germany, Italy, Japan)
- **Developing countries lag significantly** in management quality

### Management Explains TFP Differences

- A **one standard deviation improvement** in management score is associated with roughly **10–15% higher TFP**
- Management differences explain approximately **one-quarter to one-third** of cross-country TFP gaps
- Management quality is positively correlated with firm size, export status, and multinational affiliation

### Competition Drives Good Management

- Firms in more **competitive product markets** tend to have better management practices
- This suggests **market selection**: poorly managed firms are driven out by competition
- **Family-owned firms** that pass management to heirs (rather than professional managers) tend to have lower management scores
- **Multinational firms** tend to carry their management practices to foreign subsidiaries, raising host-country productivity

### Management and IT

Management quality and IT investment are **complements**:
- Firms with better management are more likely to benefit from IT investments
- This helps explain the "IT productivity paradox" of the 1980s–90s: computers were everywhere but not showing up in productivity statistics because many firms lacked the management capacity to use them effectively

## Limitations and Critiques

### [regional-bias] Western-Centric Sample and Measurement

The WMS has been criticized for potential bias:

1. **Early sample exclusion**: Initial waves focused on manufacturing in rich countries (US, UK, France, Germany, Japan). East Asian manufacturing powerhouses' alternative management models (Japanese kaizen, Korean chaebol coordination, Chinese state-enterprise systems) were underrepresented.

2. **Anglo-American management bias**: The survey instrument implicitly favors:
   - Individual performance pay over collective or seniority-based systems
   - Hard KPIs over soft qualitative assessment
   - Rapid firing of underperformers over retraining and retention
   - These preferences reflect Anglo-American HR practices, which may not be optimal in all institutional and cultural contexts.

3. **Correlation vs causation**: While the cross-sectional correlation is strong, identifying the causal effect of management on productivity requires addressing:
   - **Reverse causality**: productive firms may invest more in management
   - **Omitted variables**: unobserved CEO quality, industry conditions, or institutional environment may drive both management and productivity

### Randomized Evidence

Bloom and coauthors conducted a **randomized controlled trial** in Indian textile plants:
- 14 plants received free consulting on modern management practices
- 6 plants served as controls
- **Result**: Treated plants improved management practices and saw 17% productivity gains within the first year
- The gains persisted and even increased over time

This provides stronger causal evidence that management practices directly affect productivity, not merely correlate with it.

## In Mankiw's Textbook

Mankiw Ch 9-2 Case Study ("Management Practices and Productivity") draws on Bloom-Van Reenen's 4-country survey to argue that "management quality explains a large fraction of productivity differences." The textbook uses this as empirical support for the broader claim that cross-country income differences reflect not just factor accumulation but also "the efficiency with which capital is used."

The textbook does not discuss:
- The survey's potential Western-centric bias
- The distinction between correlation and causation in observational data
- The specific mechanisms through which management affects productivity

## 反面论点与数据空白

### [CONTRADICTION] 管理质量与制度的内生性
管理质量高的国家（美国、德国）通常也是制度质量高的国家。很难区分：
- **管理→生产率**：更好的管理直接提高生产率
- **制度→管理→生产率**：好的制度（产权、法治）使管理改进成为可能
- **文化→管理**：信任、合作文化可能同时促进好的管理和高的生产率

教材将管理质量作为独立解释变量呈现，但它可能是 deeper 制度和文化因素的代理。

### [BIAS] 管理咨询的隐含推广
Bloom-Van Reenen 的随机实验（对印度纺织厂提供免费管理咨询）显示管理改进提高生产率。但：
- **可扩展性**：14 家工厂的实验能否推广到数亿家中小企业？
- **成本效益**：管理咨询昂贵；对最穷国家，基础基础设施和健康干预可能更有 cost-effective
- **文化适应性**：从西方移植的管理实践在非西方语境中可能效果不同

### 数据空白
- **管理质量的时间序列**：管理质量是否在随时间改善？与 TFP 增长减速是否相关？
- **服务业管理**：WMS 最初聚焦制造业。服务业（占发达经济体 GDP 的 70%+）的管理质量如何测度和影响生产率？
- **公共部门管理**：政府、学校、医院的"管理质量"同样重要，但 WMS 框架主要针对私营企业
- **AI 与管理**：AI 工具（预测分析、自动化决策）可能改变"好管理"的定义。未来管理质量可能更多取决于数据基础设施和算法能力
