# 总供给曲线

The **aggregate supply (AS) curve** shows the relationship between the overall price level ($P$) and the total quantity of goods and services that firms are willing to produce ($Y$). Its shape differs fundamentally between the short run and the long run.

## The Long-Run Aggregate Supply (LRAS)

In the long run, output is determined by the economy's supply of capital, labor, and technology—not by the price level. The **LRAS curve is vertical** at the natural rate of output ($\bar{Y}$), also called potential output or full-employment output.

$$Y = \bar{Y} = F(\bar{K}, \bar{L}, \bar{A})$$

This verticality reflects the **classical dichotomy**: in the long run, real variables (output, employment) are independent of nominal variables (price level, money supply). Money is **neutral** in the long run.

The position of LRAS is determined by:
- Capital stock ($K$)
- Labor force and labor force participation ($L$)
- Technology and productivity ($A$)
- Natural rate of unemployment

## The Short-Run Aggregate Supply (SRAS)

In the short run, the **SRAS curve slopes upward**: a higher price level leads firms to produce more output. Mankiw 9e offers an intuitive explanation based on **price stickiness**:

- Some prices are fixed by contracts, menus, or implicit agreements
- When the overall price level rises above expected prices ($P > P^e$), firms with sticky prices see their real costs fall and increase production
- Conversely, when $P < P^e$, firms reduce production

A common algebraic form:

$$Y = \bar{Y} + \alpha(P - P^e)$$

where $\alpha > 0$ measures the sensitivity of output to price surprises.

## Shifts in the AS Curves

### Shifts in LRAS (Long Run)
- Technological progress → LRAS shifts right
- Capital accumulation → LRAS shifts right
- Labor force growth → LRAS shifts right
- Adverse supply shock (permanent destruction of capital) → LRAS shifts left

### Shifts in SRAS (Short Run)
- Changes in expected prices ($P^e \uparrow$) → SRAS shifts left (upward)
- Changes in input prices (oil, wages) → SRAS shifts
- Changes in labor supply or productivity → SRAS shifts

## The Adjustment from Short Run to Long Run

When $Y \neq \bar{Y}$:
- **$Y > \bar{Y}$ (boom)**: Tight labor and goods markets cause wages and prices to rise over time. $P^e$ adjusts upward, shifting SRAS left until $Y = \bar{Y}$.
- **$Y < \bar{Y}$ (recession)**: Slack markets cause wages and prices to fall (slowly, due to stickiness). $P^e$ adjusts downward, shifting SRAS right until $Y = \bar{Y}$.

This **self-correcting mechanism** operates through the adjustment of expectations and prices, but may take years.

## Microfoundations of the SRAS (Ch 14-1)

Mankiw 9e Ch 14-1 provides three distinct theories that explain why the SRAS slopes upward. All three converge on the same reduced-form equation but through different mechanisms:

### 1. Sticky Wages
Nominal wages are set by long-term contracts. When $P$ rises unexpectedly, real wages $W/P$ fall, making labor cheaper. Firms hire more workers and increase output. See [[粘性工资理论]].

### 2. Sticky Prices
Firms face menu costs when changing prices. When aggregate demand rises, firms that have not adjusted prices see increased relative demand and increase production. See [[粘性价格理论]].

### 3. Imperfect Information
Producers confuse changes in the overall price level with changes in their own product's relative price. A general price increase is partially interpreted as a favorable relative demand shift, leading to increased production. See [[不完全信息理论]].

### Comparison

| Dimension | Sticky Wages | Sticky Prices | Imperfect Information |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominal rigidity | Yes (wages) | Yes (prices) | No |
| Key friction | Contractual wage setting | Menu costs | Information asymmetry |
| Policy ineffectiveness | Temporary real effects | Temporary real effects | Only unanticipated policy matters |

For a comprehensive comparison, see [[总供给的微观基础]].

## 反面论点与数据空白

### [CONTRADICTION-1] 价格粘性的教材处理:假设而非解释

Mankiw 9e将价格粘性作为短期AS向上倾斜的**假设**,但未解释:
- **为什么价格会粘性?** 新凯恩斯主义文献提供了微基础:菜单成本(Mankiw 1985)、Calvo定价(Calvo 1983)、交错定价(Taylor 1980)、协调失灵
- **为什么工资比价格更粘性?** 效率工资、长期合同、工会力量
- **不同行业的价格粘性程度差异**:服务业价格调整远快于制造业,但教材将"价格粘性"作为同质假设

教材的"价格粘性→SRAS向上倾斜"是一个**黑箱机制**——输入假设,输出结论,中间过程不透明。

### [CONTRADICTION-2] LRAS垂直性的争议

LRAS垂直是古典二分法的产物,但存在挑战:
- **后凯恩斯主义**:长期中产出由需求决定,而非供给——存在持续的产能过剩
- **内生性增长**:需求波动可能通过干中学、研发投资等渠道影响长期潜在产出(\bar{Y}本身会移动),"长期"不是独立的
- **滞后效应**(hysteresis):长期失业会侵蚀人力资本,降低自然率本身(Blanchard-Summers 1986)

### [CONTRADICTION-3] "长期"是一个误导性概念

Keynes的著名论断"In the long run we are all dead"警示了政策含义:如果"短期"持续数十年(如日本1990-2020的停滞),"长期自动回归LRAS"对政策制定者毫无意义。教材在Ch 10-13中大量使用"长期中经济回到LRAS"的表述,但未讨论回归速度——而在现实中,回归可能需要数年至数十年。

### 数据空白

1. **价格粘性的实证测度**:Bils-Klenow (2004) 发现美国消费者价格平均调整周期约4-6个月,远短于传统假设,挑战了SRAS的斜率
2. **全球价值链与AS**:全球供应链中断(如2021芯片短缺、苏伊士运河堵塞)成为新型供给冲击,传统AS框架以国内生产为主
3. **气候变化作为AS冲击**:极端天气事件 increasingly 作为供给冲击出现,但教材2016年版未涉及
