# 政府债务与赤字

The **government budget constraint** and the dynamics of **public debt** are central to debates about fiscal sustainability, intergenerational equity, and the scope for stabilization policy. Mankiw 9e Ch 15 examines how government debt is measured, how it evolves over time, and the controversies surrounding its economic effects.

## The Government Budget Constraint

### Flow Form

$$G_t + rB_{t-1} = T_t + \Delta B_t$$

where:
- $G_t$ = government spending (excluding interest)
- $rB_{t-1}$ = interest payments on existing debt
- $T_t$ = tax revenue
- $\Delta B_t$ = new debt issued

**Intuition**: Government outlays (spending + interest) must be financed by taxes or borrowing.

### Stock Form: The Debt Dynamics Equation

Rearranging and normalizing by GDP ($Y$):

$$\frac{\Delta b}{b} = \frac{r - g}{1 + g}b_{t-1} + \frac{G - T}{Y}$$

where $b = B/Y$ is the debt-to-GDP ratio and $g$ is the GDP growth rate.

**Key insight**: The debt-to-GDP ratio evolves based on:
1. **The primary deficit** ($G - T$): new borrowing beyond interest payments
2. **The interest-growth differential** ($r - g$): if $r > g$, existing debt grows faster than the economy

## Measuring Government Debt

### Gross vs. Net Debt

- **Gross debt**: Total liabilities of the government
- **Net debt**: Gross debt minus government assets (foreign exchange reserves, state-owned enterprises, land)

### Debt Held by the Public vs. Intragovernmental

In the US:
- **Debt held by the public**: ~75% of GDP (2024); owned by domestic and foreign investors
- **Intragovernmental debt**: Social Security trust funds, etc.; ~25% of GDP
- **Total gross federal debt**: ~100% of GDP

### Historical US Debt/GDP

| Period | Debt/GDP | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1945 | 106% | WWII peak |
| 1980 | 26% | Post-war decline |
| 2008 | 68% | Pre-crisis |
| 2020 | 128% | COVID-19 response |
| 2024 | ~120% | Stabilized but elevated |

## Is Government Debt a Burden?

### The "We Owe It to Ourselves" Argument

If debt is held domestically, repayment is a **transfer** within the country, not a loss to the nation as a whole. However:
- **Distribution matters**: Debt holders (typically wealthier) receive interest paid by taxpayers (broader population)
- **Future generations**: Current borrowing may impose taxes on future citizens who did not benefit from the spending

### Crowding Out

Government borrowing can raise interest rates, reducing private investment:
- **Classical view**: Full crowding out in the long run ($S$ fixed, $I$ adjusts)
- **Keynesian view**: Partial crowding out; in recessions, government borrowing may mobilize idle savings
- **Ricardian view**: No crowding out if consumers save the tax cut (see [[Ricardian等价性]])

### Intergenerational Burden

Debt-financed spending that benefits the current generation but is repaid by future generations creates an **intergenerational transfer**:
- **War**: Deficit financing may be justified (all generations benefit from national defense)
- **Consumption**: Debt-financed transfers are harder to justify
- **Investment**: If debt finances public capital (infrastructure, education), future generations benefit

## Sustainability and Fiscal Crises

### The Debt Laffer Curve

As debt rises, the risk of default or inflation increases. Lenders demand higher interest rates, which accelerates debt growth. Beyond a certain point, additional borrowing becomes self-defeating.

### Historical Fiscal Crises

- **Greece (2010)**: Debt/GDP > 120% + loss of market confidence → sovereign debt crisis
- **Japan**: Debt/GDP > 260% but no crisis (domestic ownership, BoJ purchases)
- **US (current)**: Elevated but sustainable (reserve currency, deep markets, low $r-g$)

**The Reinhart-Rogoff controversy**: Reinhart-Rogoff (2010) claimed debt/GDP > 90% reduces growth. Herndon-Ash-Pollin (2013) found coding errors; the threshold is not robust.

## 反面论点与数据空白

### [CONTRADICTION-1] Ricardian等价的经验失效

Barro (1974) predicts debt neutrality, but:
- **2001/2008退税**: MPC ≈ 0.2-0.4 (JPS 2006)
- **liquidity constraints**: ~30% households cannot borrow
- **myopia**: Many consumers ignore future tax burdens

### [CONTRADICTION-2] $r < g$ 时代

2010s发达经济体出现 $r < g$ (利率低于增长率):
- 债务可持续条件 relax: 即使 primary deficit > 0, debt/GDP 可能 still decline
- Blanchard (2019) argues this justifies **higher sustainable debt levels**
- 但 $r < g$ 可能非永久——一旦逆转,债务动态恶化

### [CONTRADICTION-3] 货币化赤字的现代实践

Mankiw教材区分"债务融资"vs"货币融资"(铸币税),但现代央行:
- **QE**: 央行购买政府债,接近货币化
- **财政货币协调**: COVID-19期间美联储直接购买市政债
- **MMT**: 主张货币主权政府不受债务约束,通胀是唯一限制

### [CONTRADICTION-4] 隐性债务被忽略

官方债务/GDP低估了真实财政负担:
- **养老金缺口**: 现收现付制的未来承诺
- **医保成本**: 美国Medicare长期负债
- **地方政府债务**: 中国城投债、美国市政债
- **或有负债**: 存款保险、养老金担保

### 数据空白

1. **最优债务水平**: 如何在增长促进、平滑冲击、财政空间之间权衡?
2. **债务阈值**: Reinhart-Rogoff 90%阈值被推翻后,替代标准是什么?
3. **中国的政府债务**: 中央+地方+隐性(城投)的真实规模估计困难
4. **气候债务**: 绿色转型需要的公共投资 vs 现有债务约束
