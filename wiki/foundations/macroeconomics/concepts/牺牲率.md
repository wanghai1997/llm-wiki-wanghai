# 牺牲率

The **sacrifice ratio** measures the cumulative percentage of a year's real GDP that must be forgone to reduce inflation by 1 percentage point. It quantifies the real economic cost of [[反通胀的产出代价]] and is a central parameter in debates about the optimal speed of disinflation.

## Definition and Measurement

### Formula

$$\text{Sacrifice Ratio} = \frac{\sum (Y^* - Y_t)/Y^*}{\Delta \pi}$$

where:
- $Y^*$ = potential output
- $Y_t$ = actual output in period $t$
- $\Delta \pi$ = reduction in inflation (in percentage points)

### Okun's Law Transformation

Since output losses are often estimated via [[Okun法则]] ($\Delta Y/Y = -k(u - u^n)$), the sacrifice ratio can also be expressed as:

$$\text{Sacrifice Ratio} = k \times \frac{\sum (u_t - u^n)}{\Delta \pi}$$

where $k \approx 2$ (each 1% excess unemployment corresponds to ~2% output loss).

## Empirical Estimates

### US Historical Experience

Mankiw (9e) summarizes US disinflation episodes:

| Episode | Inflation Reduction | GDP Loss | Sacrifice Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1980–1983 (Volcker) | 11.3% → 3.2% | ~15% cumulative | ~5–10 |
| 1990–1994 (Greenspan "soft landing") | 6.1% → 2.6% | Minimal | ~0–2 |
| 1980s various | Various | Various | Average ~2–5 |

The wide range reflects:
- **Credibility**: The Greenspan era benefited from established anti-inflation credibility
- **Speed**: Gradual disinflation may have lower sacrifice ratios than cold turkey
- **Expectations**: Anchored expectations reduce the output cost

### Cross-Country Evidence

| Country / Period | Sacrifice Ratio | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| US (Volcker) | 5–10 | Most cited "high cost" case |
| US (1990s) | 0–2 | "Soft landing" success |
| UK (1980s) | 5–15 | Thatcher disinflation |
| Germany (1980s) | 2–4 | Bundesbank credibility |
| Canada (1990s) | 3–6 | Inflation targeting adoption |
| Emerging markets | Highly variable | Often accompanied by crises |

### Why Such Variation?

1. **Credibility and expectations**: Central banks with stronger credibility face lower sacrifice ratios
2. **Speed of disinflation**: Rapid disinflation ("cold turkey") may have higher short-run costs but faster resolution
3. **Labor market flexibility**: More flexible wages reduce the unemployment spike needed
4. **Initial inflation level**: Reducing from 20% to 10% may have different dynamics than 5% to 2%
5. **Supply shocks**: Oil price changes can confound the inflation-output relationship

## 反面论点与数据空白

### [CONTRADICTION-1] 牺牲率的美国中心主义

教材的牺牲率估计几乎全部来自美国经验,但:
- **欧洲**:德国、法国、意大利的牺牲率可能更高(因劳动市场僵化)
- **新兴市场**:阿根廷、巴西反通胀伴随剧烈社会动荡——GDP损失只是代价的一部分
- **日本**:"失去的三十年"显示,降低低通胀的"代价"可能不是衰退而是长期停滞

牺牲率概念本身假设产出损失是主要成本,忽略了社会和政治维度。

### [CONTRADICTION-2] 预期的作用被低估

教材呈现牺牲率作为"经验规律",但牺牲率高度依赖预期制度:
- **1980s**:适应性预期主导→牺牲率高
- **1990s**:通胀目标制建立→牺牲率下降
- **2010s**:前瞻性指引→牺牲率可能进一步降低

如果牺牲率不是结构参数,而是制度依赖的,教材的静态呈现误导。

### [CONTRADICTION-3] 无代价反通胀的理论vs现实

[[理性预期]]和[[无代价反通胀]]理论预测牺牲率可以为零(如果政策可信)。但:
- **可证伪性问题**:任何观察到的正牺牲率都可归因于"不可信"
- **制度摩擦**:合同、菜单成本、金融摩擦使即使可信的政策也有调整成本
- **分配效应**:即使总牺牲率低,失业集中在特定群体

### [CONTRADICTION-4] 测量困难

牺牲率的实际测量充满问题:
- **潜在产出估计**:Y*无法直接观测,不同估计方法结果差异大
- **反事实问题**:没有反通胀时的产出路径未知
- **多因素混淆**:同期财政、贸易、技术冲击影响产出
- **实时 vs 事后**:政策制定时的牺牲率估计往往事后被修正

### 数据空白

1. **牺牲率的实时估计**:政策制定时如何实时估计牺牲率?
2. **部门异质性**:反通胀的产出损失在不同行业如何分布?
3. **社会代价**:GDP损失之外的失业、不平等、健康影响如何量化?
4. **现代央行的"软着陆"**:Greenspan 1994、Powell 2024等案例的牺牲率如何?
