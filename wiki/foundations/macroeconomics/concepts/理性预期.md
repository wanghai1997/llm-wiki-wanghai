# 理性预期

**Rational expectations**, developed by [[Robert Lucas Jr]] (1972) and extended by [[Thomas Sargent]], is the hypothesis that economic agents form expectations by making optimal use of all available information, including knowledge of the economic model and policy rules. Under rational expectations, forecast errors are random and unpredictable:

$$E_t[\pi_{t+1}] = \pi_{t+1} + \varepsilon_{t+1}, \quad E_t[\varepsilon_{t+1}] = 0$$

## Core Principles

### The Rational Expectations Hypothesis

Agents do not simply extrapolate from the past ([[适应性预期]]). Instead, they:
1. Know the true structure of the economy (or a close approximation)
2. Understand current and anticipated policy rules
3. Use all available information optimally
4. Make no systematic, predictable errors

### The Policy Ineffectiveness Proposition

Lucas (1972) showed that under rational expectations:

- **Anticipated monetary policy** has no real effects
- Only **unanticipated** policy changes affect output and employment
- Systematic demand management is therefore **ineffective**

The mechanism: if the central bank follows a predictable rule (e.g., "raise money supply by 3% each year"), producers fully anticipate the resulting price level changes. They do not confuse aggregate inflation with relative price changes. Output remains at potential.

### Signal Extraction Revisited

Lucas's [[不完全信息理论]] assumes producers observe their own price $p_i$ but not the aggregate price level $P$. Under rational expectations:

$$p_i = P + (p_i - P) = \text{aggregate} + \text{relative}$$

If the monetary authority follows a rule, producers know the distribution of $P$ and optimally extract the relative price signal. Only **surprise** deviations from the rule create confusion and real effects.

## Implications for the Phillips Curve

### The Phillips Curve Disappears

Under rational expectations, the expectations-augmented Phillips curve:

$$\pi = \pi^e - \beta(u - u^n)$$

collapses. If policy is anticipated, $\pi = \pi^e$, so $u = u^n$ **always**.

Even the **short-run** tradeoff vanishes for anticipated policy.

### Credible Disinflation

[[Thomas Sargent]] (1982, "The Ends of Four Big Inflations") argued that if a central bank credibly commits to eliminating inflation, expectations adjust immediately:
- $\pi^e$ drops to the new target
- The Phillips curve shifts down without requiring a recession
- **Sacrifice ratio = 0**

This is the theoretical foundation for the [[无代价反通胀]] proposition.

## The Lucas Critique (1976)

Lucas's 1976 critique was a methodological bombshell:

> "Given that the structure of an econometric model consists of optimal decision rules of economic agents, and that optimal decision rules vary systematically with changes in the structure of series relevant to the decision maker, it follows that any change in policy will systematically alter the structure of econometric models."

**Applied to the Phillips curve**: The parameters of the Phillips curve (the slope, the natural rate) are not structural. They depend on the policy regime. If the central bank commits to low inflation, the entire Phillips curve shifts.

This destroyed the Keynesian practice of using historical estimated relationships to evaluate policy changes.

## 反面论点与数据空白

### [CONTRADICTION-1] "无代价反通胀"的经验失败

理性预期预测可信的反通胀可无代价,但:
- **Volcker反通胀(1979-82)**:美国失业率飙升至10.8%,GDP损失约12%——代价惨重
- **欧洲2010s紧缩**:尽管通胀目标从未动摇,失业率持续高企多年
- **日本2013年QQE**:央行明确承诺2%通胀目标,但通胀仅短暂上升后回落

现实表明:**可信不等于无成本**。制度变化、劳动力市场摩擦、债务通缩等因素使调整过程痛苦且缓慢。

### [CONTRADICTION-2] 共同知识假设不现实

理性假设假设所有代理人:
- 知道真实模型
- 知道其他人知道真实模型
- 知道其他人知道其他人知道...(无限递归)

这在现实中几乎从不成立:
- **调查数据**:公众通胀预期高度分散,且与理性预测存在系统性偏差
- **信息不对称**:企业、工人、消费者对经济的了解差异巨大
- **认知成本**:信息获取和处理有成本,完全理性不经济

### [CONTRADICTION-3] 行为宏观经济学的挑战

[[George Akerlof]]、[[Robert Shiller]] 等行为经济学家发现:
- **动物精神**:预期受情绪、叙事、社会传染影响,非纯粹理性计算
- **损失厌恶**:对通胀和通缩的不对称反应
- **近期偏差**:过度重视近期经验,类似适应性预期
- **异质性理性**:Angeletos-Lian (2018) 发现不完全理性预期也能解释大部分宏观数据

### [CONTRADICTION-4] 2008后的"预期失灵"

理性预期无法解释:
- **零利率下限**:当利率降至零,传统货币政策失效,但理性预期假设政策空间无限
- **长期停滞**:日本"失去的三十年"中,预期持续低迷,理性模型预测应自我纠正
- **前瞻性指引悖论**:央行承诺长期低利率→按理性预期应刺激当前支出→但效果远弱于模型预测

### 数据空白

1. **预期的异质性**:如何建模不同代理人(消费者、企业、金融家)使用不同信息集?
2. **学习动态**:从适应性到理性的过渡模型——经济如何"学会"理性?
3. **叙事经济学**:Shiller提出的"经济叙事"如何进入预期形成?
4. **中国的理性预期**:缺乏透明货币政策框架时,公众如何形成预期?
