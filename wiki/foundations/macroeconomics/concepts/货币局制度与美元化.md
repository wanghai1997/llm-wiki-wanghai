# 货币局制度与美元化

**Currency board** and **dollarization** represent the most extreme forms of fixed exchange rate regimes. They eliminate monetary policy independence entirely, offering maximum credibility at the cost of maximum rigidity.

## Currency Board

A **currency board** is a monetary arrangement in which the domestic currency is fully backed by foreign reserves and convertible into the anchor currency at a fixed rate.

### Key Features
1. **100% reserve backing**: Every unit of domestic currency is backed by foreign exchange reserves
2. **Fixed exchange rate**: Convertibility at a legally mandated rate
3. **No discretionary monetary policy**: The central bank cannot create money independently; money supply changes only through balance of payments surpluses/deficits
4. **No lender of last resort**: The central bank cannot print money to rescue banks

### Examples

| Country | Period | Anchor Currency | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hong Kong** | 1983–present | US Dollar (7.80 HKD/USD) | Survived multiple crises; no independent monetary policy |
| **Argentina** | 1991–2001 | US Dollar (1:1) | Ended hyperinflation but collapsed in 2001–2002 recession |
| **Estonia** | 1992–2011 | Deutsche Mark / Euro | Successful; adopted Euro in 2011 |
| **Bulgaria** | 1997–present | Deutsche Mark / Euro | Successful stabilization after hyperinflation |

### Advantages
- **Maximum credibility**: Eliminates speculative attacks because the peg is fully backed
- **Low inflation**: Imports the anchor country's inflation rate
- **Fiscal discipline**: Government cannot monetize deficits

### Disadvantages
- **Loss of monetary policy**: Cannot respond to domestic shocks
- **No lender of last resort**: Banking crises cannot be addressed by central bank liquidity
- **Loss of seigniorage**: The government forgoes revenue from money creation
- **Asymmetric shocks**: If the anchor country's monetary policy is inappropriate for the domestic economy, recession or overheating results

## Dollarization

**Dollarization** (or **euroization**) occurs when a country abandons its domestic currency entirely and adopts a foreign currency as legal tender.

### Types of Dollarization

**Official (de jure) dollarization**:
- The foreign currency becomes the sole legal tender
- The domestic central bank is abolished or reduced to a fiscal agency
- Examples: Ecuador (2000), El Salvador (2001), Panama (1904), Zimbabwe (2009–2019)

**Unofficial (de facto) dollarization**:
- Domestic currency remains legal tender but foreign currency dominates transactions and savings
- Common in high-inflation economies
- Examples: Peru, Cambodia, Vietnam (to varying degrees)

### Advantages
- **Maximum credibility**: Complete elimination of currency risk
- **Lower transaction costs**: Eliminates exchange rate spreads and hedging costs
- **Deep integration**: Facilitates trade and investment with the anchor country

### Disadvantages
- **Complete loss of monetary policy and seigniorage**
- **No lender of last resort**: Banking crises require fiscal resources or external support
- **Asymmetric shocks**: No exchange rate adjustment to cushion shocks
- **Political symbolism**: Loss of monetary sovereignty carries political costs

## Case Studies

### Argentina's Currency Board (1991–2001)

**Success phase (1991–1998)**:
- Ended hyperinflation (from 3,000% to near zero)
- Restored confidence and investment
- GDP grew rapidly

**Failure phase (1998–2001)**:
- Brazilian devaluation (1999) made Argentine exports uncompetitive
- US monetary tightening was inappropriate for recessionary Argentina
- Fiscal deficits accumulated; debt/GDP rose
- Banking panic in 2001; deposit freezes ("corralito")
- Collapse in December 2001: default, devaluation, social unrest

**Lessons**:
- Currency boards can stop hyperinflation but create new vulnerabilities
- Without fiscal discipline, even a currency board cannot prevent crisis
- The cost of exit is catastrophic

### Hong Kong's Currency Board (1983–present)

**Success factors**:
- Strong fiscal position (large reserves, no public debt)
- Flexible factor markets (wages and prices adjust)
- Prudent financial regulation
- Integration with China's economy provides growth

**Challenges**:
- US monetary policy often inappropriate for Hong Kong's economic cycle
- Asset price bubbles fueled by low US rates
- 1997 Asian crisis tested the peg; defended successfully but at the cost of deep deflation (1998–2003)

## 反面论点与数据空白

### [CONTRADICTION-1] 货币局的成功需要配套条件

教材将货币局作为"可信固定汇率"的案例,但成功需要:
- **财政纪律**:阿根廷失败的主因是财政赤字
- **劳动力市场灵活**:香港工资和价格的向下灵活性帮助调整
- **强大的银行监管**:没有最后贷款人,银行体系必须格外稳健
- **政治承诺**:退出成本极高,需要不可逆转的政治承诺

这些条件在多数发展中国家不具备,使货币局的推广风险极高。

### [CONTRADICTION-2] 美元化的不可逆性被低估

教材未充分讨论美元化的退出成本:
- **厄瓜多尔(2000)**:美元化后经历多次银行业危机,无法通过货币政策应对
- **萨尔瓦多(2001)**:2021年成为首个将比特币定为法定货币的国家,部分原因是对美元化束缚的不满
- **津巴布韦(2009-2019)**:先美元化制止通胀,后试图恢复本币(2019),立即引发通胀飙升

退出美元化的制度和政治成本极高,教材对此讨论不足。

### [CONTRADICTION-3] "锚货币"选择的美国偏见

教材案例几乎全部以美元为锚货币,但:
- **欧元区**:东欧国家钉住欧元(保加利亚、丹麦)
- **法郎区**:非洲 CFA 法郎区钉住欧元(前法国殖民地)
- **澳大利亚/新西兰**:太平洋岛国钉住澳元或纽元

美元并非唯一选择,锚货币的选择取决于贸易和金融联系。教材的美式偏见使"美元化"成为通用术语,忽略了其他锚货币安排。

### 数据空白

1. **最优货币区理论**:Mundell (1961) 指出,货币联盟(或固定汇率)要求劳动力流动、价格灵活、财政转移——但这些条件在多数货币局国家不具备
2. **数字货币与货币替代**:CBDC可能使"数字美元化"更容易(如柬埔寨的Bakong系统)
3. **加密货币作为替代**:萨尔瓦多2021年采用比特币,挑战传统"美元化"概念
4. **后疫情时代的汇率制度选择**:2020后全球不确定性增加,汇率制度选择是否正在变化?
