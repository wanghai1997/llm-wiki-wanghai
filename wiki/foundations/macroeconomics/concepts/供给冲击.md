# 供给冲击

A **supply shock** is an event that directly alters firms' costs of production or the economy's productive capacity, shifting the [[总供给曲线]]. Unlike demand shocks, supply shocks create the painful tradeoff of **stagflation**—simultaneous recession and inflation.

## Types of Supply Shocks

### Adverse Supply Shocks (SRAS shifts left)

**Energy price shocks**
- 1973 OPEC oil embargo: oil prices quadrupled
- 1979 Iranian revolution: oil prices doubled
- 1990 Gulf War: temporary oil price spike
- 2022 Russia-Ukraine war: natural gas and oil disruption

**Wage-push shocks**
- Unionization drives (1930s US, 1970s UK)
- Minimum wage increases
- Labor market regulations

**Natural disasters and pandemics**
- COVID-19 (2020): global supply chain disruption
- Hurricane Katrina (2005): US Gulf Coast energy infrastructure
- Japanese earthquake/tsunami (2011): auto parts and electronics shortages

**Regulatory and tax shocks**
- New environmental regulations
- Payroll tax increases
- Trade restrictions raising input costs

### Favorable Supply Shocks (SRAS shifts right)

**Technological breakthroughs**
- IT revolution (1990s): productivity surge, low inflation with high growth
- Fracking revolution (2005–2015): US oil and gas boom

**Commodity price collapses**
- 1986 oil price collapse: from $30 to $10/barrel
- 2014–2016 oil price collapse: from $100 to $30/barrel

**Trade liberalization**
- China's WTO accession (2001): global manufacturing cost reduction
- NAFTA (1994): North American supply chain integration

## Effects in the AD-AS Framework

### Adverse Supply Shock

```
Price Level (P)
    │
    │    SRAS₁  SRAS₂
    │      /      /
    │     /     /
    │    /    /
    │   /   /     LRAS
    │  /  /        │
    │ / /          │
    └─●────────────│─────→ Output (Y)
      Y₂  Ȳ
```

- **Short run**: Output falls ($Y_2 < \bar{Y}$), price level rises ($P_2 > P_1$)
- **Stagflation**: The worst of both worlds—recession + inflation
- **Policy dilemma**: Stimulating AD to raise $Y$ worsens inflation; restricting AD to fight inflation deepens recession

### Favorable Supply Shock

- Output rises, price level falls
- The "Goldilocks" scenario of non-inflationary growth
- Example: US late 1990s (IT productivity boom)

## Accommodating vs. Non-Accommodating Policy Response

When faced with an adverse supply shock, policymakers face a stark choice:

**Accommodating policy** (shift AD right):
- Accept higher inflation to prevent deep recession
- Used by many central banks in 1970s (result: persistent high inflation)

**Non-accommodating policy** (maintain AD):
- Accept recession to prevent inflation from spiraling
- Volcker Fed 1979–1982: tight monetary policy broke inflation but caused deep recession
- Sacrifice ratio: ~2% output loss for each 1% reduction in inflation

## 反面论点与数据空白

### [CONTRADICTION-1] 供给冲击的"纯粹性"存疑

教材将供给冲击作为外生事件,但许多所谓"供给冲击"实际上有需求侧根源:
- **1970s石油危机**:OPEC提价本身是对西方需求增长的反应,也是地缘政治决策
- **2021-2022供应链危机**:疫情需求结构突变(服务→商品)是主要驱动力,而非纯粹的生产中断
- **中国清零政策(2022)**:国内防疫政策同时抑制供给和需求

将冲击简单归类为"供给"或"需求"可能掩盖其深层机制。

### [CONTRADICTION-2] 滞胀的教材处理过时

教材以1970s石油危机为滞胀的主要案例,但2010s后:
- 发达经济体多次经历"低通胀+低增长"(日本化),而非"高通胀+低增长"
- 2021-2022出现了供给冲击驱动的通胀,但央行迅速加息而非容忍——与1970s"适应型政策"不同
- 现代通胀更多来自财政刺激+供应链瓶颈的混合,而非纯供给冲击

### [CONTRADICTION-3] 忽略气候变化的供给冲击

教材2016年版未将气候变化作为系统性供给冲击来源。但:
- 极端天气 increasingly 破坏农业产出和基础设施
- 能源转型(碳中和)构成永久性供给约束
- 气候移民改变劳动力供给
- Stern Review (2006) 估计气候变化可能造成5-20%的GDP损失

这是教材知识缺口中最重要的之一。

### 数据空白

1. **供给冲击的量化困难**:如何区分成本推动型通胀 vs 需求拉动型通胀?统计方法(如SVAR)存在识别争议
2. **全球价值链放大了冲击**:中间品贸易使局部供给冲击全球化——2011泰国洪水导致全球硬盘短缺
3. **自然利率与供给冲击**:负向供给冲击可能降低自然利率r*,使货币政策空间进一步收窄
