# 永久收入假说

The **permanent income hypothesis (PIH)**, developed by [[Milton Friedman]] in *A Theory of the Consumption Function* (1957), argues that consumption depends not on current income but on **permanent income** — the present value of expected lifetime resources. Temporary income changes have little effect on consumption because consumers smooth them over time.

## The Core Idea

### Decomposing Income

Friedman proposed that observed income ($Y$) has two components:

$$Y = Y^p + Y^t$$

- **Permanent income** ($Y^p$): The long-run average income a consumer expects to receive; reflects human capital, skills, career prospects
- **Transitory income** ($Y^t$): Temporary deviations from permanent income; can be positive (bonus, overtime) or negative (layoff, illness)

### The Consumption Function

$$C = k Y^p$$

where $k$ depends on the interest rate, time preference, and the ratio of non-human to human wealth.

**Key predictions**:
- **MPC out of permanent income**: $\approx k$ (close to 1)
- **MPC out of transitory income**: $\approx 0$ (saved or used to pay down debt)

### Solving the Consumption Puzzle

Friedman's PIH (together with Modigliani's LCH) resolves Kuznets's **consumption puzzle**:

| Data type | Observation | PIH Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **Short-run cross-section** | High-income households have higher saving rates | High observed income often includes positive transitory components |
| **Short-run time series** | MPC ≈ 0.9 | Year-to-year income changes are partly transitory |
| **Long-run time series** | MPC ≈ 1.0 | Over long periods, transitory components average to zero |

## Permanent Income and Expectations

### Adaptive Expectations

Friedman originally assumed consumers form $Y^p$ using **adaptive expectations**:

$$Y^p_t = Y^p_{t-1} + \lambda(Y_t - Y^p_{t-1})$$

- $\lambda \in (0,1)$: speed of adjustment
- Consumers gradually update their estimate of permanent income based on observed income

### Rational Expectations Extension

[[Robert Hall]] (1978) combined PIH with rational expectations to derive the **random walk hypothesis**: if consumers use all available information optimally, consumption changes should be unpredictable (see [[随机游走假说]]).

## Empirical Evidence

### Supporting Evidence

- **Tax rebates**: Studies (JPS 2006, Sahm et al. 2010) find MPC out of predictable tax rebates is ~0.2-0.4, consistent with PIH (not 1.0 as Keynes would predict)
- **Consumption smoothing**: Consumers appear to maintain stable consumption through temporary income shocks (unemployment, medical expenses)
- **Wealth effects**: Stock market wealth (more permanent) has larger consumption effects than temporary income shocks

### Challenges

- **Excess sensitivity (Flavin 1981)**: Consumption responds more to **predictable** income changes than PIH allows
- **Liquidity constraints**: ~30% of households cannot borrow; they must consume current income regardless of permanent income
- **Rule-of-thumb consumers**: Some fraction of households simply spend current income (Campbell-Mankiw 1989 estimated ~50%)
- **Information limitations**: Consumers may not know their permanent income with precision

## Comparison with the Life-Cycle Hypothesis

| Dimension | Permanent Income Hypothesis | Life-Cycle Hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| **Time horizon** | Infinite (or very long) | Finite (lifetime) |
| **Key variable** | $Y^p$ = expected long-run income | Age and retirement planning |
| **Wealth role** | Human + non-human wealth | Accumulation for retirement |
| **Aggregate saving** | Depends on income uncertainty | Depends on demographics and growth |
| **Empirical focus** | Time-series consumption smoothing | Cross-section age profiles |

The two theories are **complementary**: PIH explains how consumers respond to income uncertainty; LCH explains how age and retirement affect saving. Modern consumption theory combines both.

## 反面论点与数据空白

### [CONTRADICTION-1] 过度敏感与过度平滑并存

Flavin (1981) "excess sensitivity": consumption responds too much to predictable income changes. Campbell-Deaton (1989) "excess smoothness": consumption responds too little to permanent shocks.这两个发现看似矛盾,但可能反映:
- **部分信息**: 消费者无法完美区分永久与暂时冲击
- **习惯形成**: 过去消费影响当前效用,产生惯性
- **异质性**: 不同群体的行为不同,aggregate结果复杂

### [CONTRADICTION-2] 流动性约束的普遍性

PIH假设完美资本市场,但:
- **美国**: ~30%家庭无法获得信贷
- **发展中国家**: 信贷市场极不发达
- **2008危机**: 住房财富(HELOC)冻结使中产阶级也受约束

 liquidity-constrained households的行为完全由当期收入驱动,与PIH矛盾。

### [CONTRADICTION-3] 心理账户与框架效应

Thaler (1999)的心理账户理论显示:
- 消费者将收入分入不同"账户"(工资、奖金、退税)
- 不同账户的MPC不同(退税MPC > 工资MPC)
- 这与PIH的"所有收入同质"假设矛盾

### [CONTRADICTION-4] 行为偏差的系统性

Laibson的present bias、Thaler的心理账户、Kahneman的前景理论都显示:
- 消费者不是完全理性的跨期优化者
- 自我控制问题、注意力限制、社会比较均影响消费
- PIH作为基准模型仍然有用,但需行为修正

### 数据空白

1. **永久收入的实时识别**: 消费者如何在政策制定时点区分永久与暂时收入?
2. **异质性PIH**: 不同财富/年龄/教育群体的PIH适用性差异
3. **中国的永久收入**: 户籍制度、城乡差距、社会保障不足使永久收入极难预测
4. **数字支付数据**: 高频交易数据能否改善永久收入估计?
