# 内生增长理论

**Endogenous growth theory** addresses the most unsatisfying feature of the [[Solow增长模型]]: its treatment of technological progress as an **exogenous** parameter $g$ ("manna from heaven"). Endogenous growth models explain long-run growth as the outcome of deliberate economic decisions—particularly investment in knowledge, human capital, and R&D.

## Motivation: The Solow Model's Limitation

In the Solow model with technological progress:
- Long-run per-capita growth equals $g$
- $g$ is **exogenous**: unexplained by the model
- Policy affects the **level** of the balanced growth path but not its **slope**

This is empirically and theoretically unsatisfying: policies clearly affect innovation (patent laws, R&D subsidies, education), and different countries have sustained different growth rates for extended periods.

## Major Endogenous Growth Models

### 1. The AK Model (Simplest Endogenous Growth)

The simplest endogenous growth model drops the assumption of diminishing returns to capital:

$$Y = AK$$

where $A$ is a constant technology parameter.

Capital accumulation:
$$\Delta K = sY - \delta K = sAK - \delta K$$

Per-capita growth rate:
$$\frac{\Delta y}{y} = sA - \delta$$

**Key result**: Growth is **permanent** and depends on policy parameters ($s$, $A$). There is no steady state in per-capita terms—the economy grows forever.

**Interpretation**: "Capital" is broadly defined to include physical capital, human capital, and knowledge. The assumption of constant returns to this broad capital stock is extreme but captures the intuition that knowledge accumulation may not face diminishing returns.

**Limitation**: The AK model is a "black box." It asserts non-diminishing returns without microfoundations. It cannot explain why growth rates differ across countries with similar policies.

### 2. The Two-Sector Model (Uzawa-Lucas)

A more structured approach divides the economy into:
- **Goods sector**: Produces output using physical capital and human capital
- **Education sector**: Produces human capital using existing human capital

If human capital accumulation has constant returns to scale in human capital, perpetual growth is possible. [[Robert Lucas Jr]] (1988) showed that human capital externalities (spillovers from educated workers) can sustain growth even without exogenous technology.

### 3. Romer (1990): R&D-Based Growth

[[Paul Romer]]'s canonical model (see [[Paul Romer]] and [[研发微观基础]]) introduces a third sector:
- **Final goods**: Competitive, uses labor, capital, and designs
- **Intermediate goods**: Monopolistically competitive, buys designs and produces specialized capital
- **R&D**: Produces new designs using human capital; designs are nonrival

Key features:
- **Ideas are nonrival**: A design can be used by many firms at zero marginal cost
- **Partial excludability**: Patents grant temporary monopoly power
- **"Standing on shoulders"**: New knowledge builds on old knowledge

The growth rate depends on the stock of human capital devoted to R&D. Policy can affect long-run growth by subsidizing education and R&D.

### 4. Aghion-Howitt (1992): Schumpeterian Creative Destruction

Philippe Aghion and Peter Howitt formalized [[Joseph Schumpeter]]'s concept of **creative destruction**:
- Firms conduct R&D to discover the next generation of technology
- Successful innovators drive incumbents out of business
- Growth is driven by the **frequency of innovations**, which depends on R&D labor

**Key difference from Romer**: In Aghion-Howitt, innovation **destroys** old technologies; in Romer, innovation **adds to** the stock of knowledge. This leads to different predictions about:
- The optimal patent length (shorter in Schumpeterian models due to business-stealing externalities)
- The relationship between competition and innovation (inverted-U in Aghion-Howitt)
- The welfare effects of R&D (potentially excessive due to duplication)

## Common Themes

All endogenous growth models share:
1. **Intentional innovation**: Growth results from deliberate decisions to invest in knowledge
2. **Some form of non-convexity**: Constant returns to knowledge/human capital at the aggregate level, despite diminishing returns to individual inputs
3. **Policy relevance**: Long-run growth rates depend on policy (education, R&D, patents, trade)
4. **Externalities**: Innovation creates spillovers not captured by private investors, justifying public intervention

## Scale Effects Debate

Early endogenous growth models (Romer 1990, first-generation) featured **strong scale effects**: larger populations → more innovators → faster growth. This was criticized because:
- Global population grew enormously in the 20th century, but growth rates did not rise proportionally
- China and India did not grow faster than smaller countries simply because they are large

**Second-generation models** (Jones 1995, "semi-endogenous growth") assume "fishing out" (ideas get harder to find as the stock grows). In these models:
- Long-run growth depends on the **growth rate** of population, not its **level**
- Policy can affect the **level** of income but not the long-run growth rate (unless it affects population growth)
- This is a partial return to Solow-style exogeneity

## In Mankiw's Textbook

Mankiw Ch 9-4 presents endogenous growth theory as the culmination of the growth theory narrative:
- The AK model as the simplest illustration
- Romer's R&D model as the primary framework
- Aghion-Howitt creative destruction as an important alternative
- Policy implications (R&D subsidies, education, patents)

The textbook does not deeply explore:
- The scale effects debate (Jones 1995)
- Empirical tests of endogenous growth predictions
- The political economy of innovation policy (who captures R&D subsidies?)

## 反面论点与数据空白

### [CONTRADICTION] 内生增长的经验验证困难
教材将内生增长理论呈现为 Solow 模型的"自然延伸"和"解决方案"，但不讨论其经验困难：
- **规模效应预测失败**：第一代模型预测大国增长更快，这与数据不符
- **R&D-增长关联不稳定**：R&D 支出与增长的相关性在不同样本和时期差异巨大
- **政策实验稀缺**：很少有可信的准实验表明 R&D 补贴显著提高了长期增长率

### [BIAS] "创新=增长"的线性叙事
教材将增长理论史呈现为从外生到内生的线性进步：Solow 发现问题 → Romer 解决问题。但：
- 内生增长模型引入了新的问题（规模效应、R&D 定价、专利悖论）
- "半内生增长"（Jones 1995）实际上部分回到了外生框架
- 知识生产的"生产函数"缺乏微观基础， calibration 高度 arbitrary

### 数据空白
- **Jones (1995) 半内生增长**：这一重要修正完全未在教材中出现。Jones 证明如果 ideas 越来越难发现（"站在肩膀上" but "站在脚趾上"），长期增长率取决于人口增长率而非 R&D 投入
- **创新政策的政治经济学**：R&D 补贴往往被在位企业捕获，用于防御性专利而非真正的创新。教材未讨论这一"寻租"维度
- **全球创新分工**：现代创新是全球化的（iPhone 设计在美国、制造在中国、组件来自日韩），标准国家层面的内生增长模型难以捕捉这一分工
- **AI 与增长**：AI 是否改变了知识生产的 returns to scale？如果 AI 可以自动化 R&D，内生增长模型的基本假设可能需要重写
