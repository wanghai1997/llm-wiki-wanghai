# 投资理论

**The microfoundations of investment** trace how economists model firms' capital accumulation decisions—moving from the simple heuristic that "investment depends on interest rates" to explicit optimization frameworks that incorporate expectations, financial frictions, and asset market valuations. Mankiw 9e Ch 17 presents three complementary models, each addressing a different dimension of real-world investment behavior.

## The Three Models: A Hierarchy

```
Neoclassical Model (Jorgenson 1963) → Firms rent capital to maximize profit
    ↓
Tobin's q Theory (1969)            → Stock market signals investment via q ratio
    ↓
Financial Constraints (Fazzari-Hubbard-Petersen 1988) → Credit market imperfections matter
    ↓
Housing & Inventory Investment     → Sector-specific applications
```

## Model 1: The Neoclassical Investment Model

Firms rent capital at rental rate $R$ and face output price $p$:

$$MPK = R = (r + \delta)P$$

The desired capital stock $K^*$ satisfies $MPK = r + \delta$ (where $r$ is the real interest rate and $\delta$ is depreciation).

**Investment is the adjustment to desired capital**:

$$I = K^* - K_{-1} + \delta K_{-1}$$

Key implications:
- Investment depends negatively on $r$ (higher rates raise capital cost)
- Investment depends positively on productivity shifts (MPK schedule shifts)
- With convex adjustment costs (Hayashi 1982), investment is smoothed over time

See [[新古典投资模型]]

## Model 2: Tobin's q Theory

**Tobin's q** links financial markets to real investment:

$$q = \frac{\text{Market value of installed capital}}{\text{Replacement cost of capital}}$$

- **$q > 1$**: Market values capital above cost → invest
- **$q < 1$**: Market values capital below cost → do not invest

Forward-looking advantage: $q$ incorporates expectations about future profitability.

See [[Tobin q理论]]

## Model 3: Financial Constraints

When capital markets are imperfect:
- **External finance premium**: Asymmetric information raises the cost of external vs. internal funds
- **Cash flow sensitivity**: Investment depends on cash flow, especially for small firms
- **Collateral constraints**: Net worth determines borrowing capacity

The financial accelerator: negative shocks → lower net worth → reduced investment → further output decline.

## Housing and Inventory Investment

### Housing Investment
- Durable consumption + investment hybrid
- Highly interest-sensitive: mortgage rates drive demand
- 2000s boom/bust: housing was the epicenter of financial instability

### Inventory Investment
- Production buffer: firms hold inventories to smooth production
- Accelerator model: $I_{inv} = a \cdot \Delta Y$
- JIT revolution: reduced inventory/GDP ratio, but 2020 supply chain shock showed vulnerabilities

## 反面论点与数据空白

### [CONTRADICTION-1] q理论的经验弱化

q explains only 10-20% of investment variation (Blanchard-Rhee-Summers 1993). Cash flow (reflecting financial constraints) often dominates q in empirical tests. This suggests the neoclassical framework—while elegant—misses the dominant driver of investment fluctuations.

### [CONTRADICTION-2] "动物精神"的不可建模性

Keynes (1936) argued investment is driven by "animal spirits"—waves of optimistic and pessimistic expectations that are not reducible to optimization. The post-2008 investment slump persisted despite low interest rates and high q, challenging any purely mechanical investment model.

### [CONTRADICTION-3] 微观-宏观桥接失败

Micro investment theory predicts smooth adjustment to optimal capital. Macro data shows:
- **Lumpy investment**: Firms make discrete, large adjustments (not continuous smoothing)
- **Irreversibility**: Once installed, capital is hard to resell → option value of waiting matters
- **Aggregate inertia**: Firm-level lumpy investment does not cancel out in aggregate (Doms-Dunne 1998)

### 数据空白

1. **不确定性冲击的投资效应**: Bloom (2009)发现政策不确定性使投资大幅下降;标准模型不处理"等待的期权价值"
2. **数字资本的投资测度**: 软件、数据、AI 基础设施的折旧率和回报率缺乏可靠估计
3. **气候投资**: 绿色转型所需的投资规模(~$1-2T/年全球)是否由市场自发完成,还是需要政策干预
