# Trevor Swan

**Trevor Winchester Swan** (1918–1989), Australian economist at the Australian National University (ANU), independently derived the neoclassical growth model in 1956, the same year as [[Robert Solow]]'s more widely cited publication.

## Independent Discovery

Swan's paper *"Economic Growth and Capital Accumulation"* (Economic Record, 1956) presented essentially the same model as Solow: a neoclassical production function with diminishing returns to capital, embedded in a dynamic framework where savings fund capital accumulation until the economy reaches a steady state. The two papers were written contemporaneously without either author being aware of the other's work.

While Solow's QJE paper became the canonical citation in the Anglo-American economics tradition, Swan's contribution was recognized in Australian and Commonwealth academic circles. The model is now sometimes referred to as the **Solow-Swan model** to acknowledge this dual discovery.

## Why the Asymmetry?

Swan's relative obscurity in standard textbook treatments reflects several factors:
- Solow published in the higher-profile *Quarterly Journal of Economics* (Harvard/MIT), while Swan published in the *Economic Record* (Australian)
- Solow's subsequent 1957 growth accounting paper and long career at MIT built a cumulative research program around the model
- Solow received the 1987 Nobel Prize, cementing his name in the canonical history

Mankiw's textbook mentions Swan only in a footnote (Ch 8), though the footnote does explicitly note the independent discovery.
