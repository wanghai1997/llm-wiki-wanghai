# John Van Reenen

**John Van Reenen** (b. 1964), economist at MIT Sloan School of Management, is a leading researcher on innovation, productivity, and management practices. He is best known for co-developing the **World Management Survey** with [[Nicholas Bloom]], which produced the empirical evidence on management quality and productivity featured in Mankiw's textbook.

## World Management Survey

Van Reenen and Bloom launched the World Management Survey (WMS) in the early 2000s to address a critical gap in empirical economics: while management was widely believed to matter for firm performance, it had rarely been measured systematically across firms and countries. The WMS uses a structured interview protocol (typically double-blind, with independent surveyors scoring management practices from 1 to 5) to quantify management quality.

### Key Research Findings (with Bloom and collaborators)

- **Management matters**: A one standard deviation improvement in management score is associated with roughly 10-15% higher total factor productivity
- **The US management premium**: American firms score highest among early surveyed countries, partly due to more competitive product markets and better allocation of talent to management roles
- **Management and trade**: Multinational firms tend to carry their management practices abroad; exporting firms have better management than non-exporters
- **IT and management complementarity**: Firms with better management are more likely to benefit from IT investments, suggesting that "IT productivity paradox" findings reflect management quality differences

## Research Beyond Management

Van Reenen's broader research agenda includes:
- **Innovation policy**: Evaluating R&D tax credits, patent systems, and their effects on innovation
- **Firm dynamics**: Entry, exit, and reallocation of resources across firms
- **Inequality**: The relationship between technological change, firm organization, and wage dispersion
- **Brexit and economic policy**: Assessing the economic consequences of the UK's departure from the EU

## In Mankiw's Textbook

Mankiw Ch 9-2 Case Study ("Management Practices and Productivity") draws on Bloom-Van Reenen's 4-country survey (US, UK, France, Germany) to argue that "management quality explains a large fraction of productivity differences across firms and countries." The textbook uses this as empirical support for the broader claim that "efficiency with which capital is used" (not just factor accumulation) drives cross-country income differences.
