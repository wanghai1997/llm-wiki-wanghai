# Finn Kydland

**Finn Erling Kydland** (b. 1943) is a Norwegian-American economist at the University of California, Santa Barbara, awarded the **2004 Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences** jointly with [[Edward Prescott]] "for their contributions to dynamic macroeconomics: the time consistency of economic policy and the driving forces behind business cycles." Kydland is the co-founder of the **Real Business Cycle (RBC)** paradigm and the **time inconsistency** framework in macroeconomic policy.

## Real Business Cycle Theory (1982)

### The Kydland-Prescott Model

In *"Time to Build and Aggregate Fluctuations"* (Econometrica, 1982), Kydland and Prescott launched the RBC revolution. Their central claim: **business cycles are driven by real (technology) shocks, not monetary or demand shocks**.

**Key features**:
- **Microfoundations**: Built from optimizing representative agents with rational expectations
- **Technology shocks**: Solow residual (TFP) fluctuations are the source of cycles
- **Market clearing**: No nominal rigidities; prices and wages adjust instantly
- **Calibration, not estimation**: Parameters set to match long-run growth facts, not short-run dynamics

**The production side**:
$$Y_t = A_t F(K_t, L_t)$$
where $A_t$ follows a stochastic process (the technology shock).

**The mechanism**: A positive technology shock raises productivity → higher real wages → more labor supply (intertemporal substitution) → higher output and investment → boom. A negative shock produces recession.

### "Time to Build" and Investment Dynamics

Kydland-Prescott emphasized that **investment projects take time to complete**. Multi-period capital accumulation creates **propagation mechanisms**: a shock today affects investment decisions for several periods, generating persistence in output fluctuations.

### The RBC Debate

**Strengths**:
- Provided rigorous microfoundations for macroeconomics (responding to the Lucas critique)
- Forced Keynesian models to also adopt optimization-based foundations
- Accounted for co-movement of output, consumption, investment, and labor

**Weaknesses**:
- Cannot explain unemployment (model assumes continuous market clearing)
- Technology shock identification is questionable (Solow residual includes measurement error, demand shocks)
- Predicts consumption should be more volatile than output (empirically, consumption is smoother)
- Labor market predictions weak: requires implausibly high intertemporal elasticity of labor supply

## Time Inconsistency (1977)

### The Core Problem

In *"Rules Rather Than Discretion: The Inconsistency of Optimal Plans"* (Journal of Political Economy, 1977, with Prescott), Kydland showed a fundamental problem with **discretionary policymaking**:

**The intuition**: A policymaker announces an optimal plan (e.g., zero inflation). Private agents form expectations based on this announcement. But once expectations are set, the policymaker has an incentive to renege (e.g., surprise inflation to boost output). Rational agents anticipate this, so the announced plan is not credible.

**The inflation bias**:
- Society's optimal inflation: $\pi^* = 0$
- With discretion and a short-run Phillips curve tradeoff: the equilibrium inflation is $\bar{\pi} > 0$
- Result: **higher inflation with no output gain**

### Policy Implications

1. **Commitment devices**: Central bank independence, inflation targeting, fixed rules (Taylor rule)
2. **Reputation**: Repeated interaction can sustain low inflation if the cost of losing reputation is high
3. **Institutional design**: The structure of policymaking matters as much as the identity of policymakers

### Connection to Lucas Critique

Time inconsistency is an application of the [[Lucas批判]]: policy evaluation must account for how private-sector expectations respond to policy regimes. Under discretion, the "optimal" policy is not optimal because it changes the economic environment (expectations) in a way that undermines itself.

## 反面论点与数据空白

- **RBC的经验失败**: RBC模型在2008金融危机后急剧边缘化。金融加速器、信贷摩擦、 nominal rigidities 被证明是理解波动的关键——而RBC全部排除。 calibration方法(设定参数而非估计)使模型免于统计检验,被批评为"不可证伪"。
- **时间不一致性的过度推广**: 时间不一致性框架被用来反对几乎所有相机抉择政策,但现实中:
  - 规则本身也可能时间不一致(如Taylor规则在极端冲击下需要调整)
  - 完全承诺不可行(未来不确定性)
  - 最优化问题可能太复杂,无法预先承诺
- **代表性代理人假设**: RBC和time inconsistency模型都假设单一代表性代理人,忽略了异质性。HANK模型(异质性代理人新凯恩斯)显示,不同收入/财富群体的政策响应差异巨大,aggregate结果与representative agent模型截然不同。
- **regional-bias**: RBC以美国战后数据为校准基准,对发展中国家(制度不稳定、金融不发达、外部冲击主导)适用性极弱。
