# Alvin Hansen

**Alvin Harvey Hansen** (1887–1975), Harvard economist, was the foremost American exponent of Keynesian economics in the mid-20th century. Known as the "American Keynes," Hansen played a pivotal role in translating and institutionalizing Keynes's ideas in the United States.

## Core Contributions

### IS-LM Popularization and the Neoclassical Synthesis

Hansen, together with [[John Hicks]], helped formalize the [[IS-LM模型]] as the standard pedagogical and policy framework for Keynesian analysis. His 1953 textbook *A Guide to Keynes* became the definitive exposition for American graduate students. Unlike Hicks's original two-diagram presentation, Hansen developed the now-standard four-quadrant derivation of the IS and LM curves.

### Secular Stagnation (1939)

Hansen introduced the concept of **secular stagnation**—a prolonged period of deficient aggregate demand due to structural factors:
- Declining population growth
- Closing of the American frontier
- Reduced scope for transformative technological innovation
- Weakened investment opportunities

This thesis was largely dismissed during the post-war boom but experienced a revival after the 2008 financial crisis, most notably in the work of Larry Summers (2013).

### Fiscal Policy Advocacy

Hansen was an early and forceful advocate for **compensatory fiscal policy**—using deficit spending during recessions and surpluses during booms. He influenced the Full Employment Act of 1946 and the Council of Economic Advisers under Presidents Truman and Kennedy.

## Relationship to Mankiw's Framework

Mankiw does not explicitly name Hansen in Ch 10-13, but the entire IS-LM apparatus that Mankiw teaches is essentially the Hansen-Hicks synthesis. Hansen's secular stagnation thesis also anticipates the modern discussion of persistent demand shortfalls and the [[流动性陷阱]].
