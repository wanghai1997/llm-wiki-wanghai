# Ben Bernanke

**Ben Shalom Bernanke** (b. 1953) is an American economist who served as **Chairman of the Federal Reserve** from 2006 to 2014. His academic work on the **financial accelerator** and the **macroeconomic effects of credit market frictions** became the theoretical backbone for understanding the 2008 financial crisis and shaped the unprecedented policy response (quantitative easing, forward guidance, bank stress tests).

## Academic Contributions

### The Financial Accelerator (Bernanke-Gertler-Gilchrist, 1999)

Bernanke's most influential academic contribution, developed with Mark Gertler and Simon Gilchrist, is the **financial accelerator** model. It formalizes how **credit market imperfections amplify economic fluctuations**:

**The mechanism**:
1. A negative shock reduces firms' net worth and collateral values
2. With asymmetric information, external finance is more costly than internal finance
3. Lower net worth → higher external finance premium → reduced investment
4. Reduced investment → lower output → further declines in asset prices and net worth
5. **Feedback loop**: The initial shock is amplified through the credit channel

**Formal expression**:
$$I = I(q, \text{net worth}), \quad \frac{\partial I}{\partial \text{net worth}} > 0$$

This model explains why:
- Small shocks can cause large recessions
- The 2008 crisis was so severe (housing price collapse → bank losses → credit freeze)
- Recovery from financial crises is typically slow (net worth rebuilds gradually)

### Bernanke (1983): Non-Monetary Effects of the Financial Crisis

In his early work on the Great Depression, Bernanke argued that the **banking panics of 1930–33** had effects beyond the monetary contraction that Friedman and Schwartz emphasized:
- Destruction of credit relationships
- Loss of financial intermediation services
- Prolonged recovery due to the time needed to rebuild banking relationships

This "credit view" complement to the "money view" shaped his crisis-response philosophy in 2008.

## Federal Reserve Chairmanship (2006–2014)

### The 2008 Financial Crisis

Bernanke took office in February 2006, just as the housing bubble was peaking. His response to the crisis was shaped by his academic research:

**Key policy actions**:
- **Emergency lending**: Extending Fed lending facilities beyond depository institutions (investment banks, money market funds, AIG)
- **Quantitative Easing (QE)**: Large-scale asset purchases (Treasuries, MBS) to lower long-term interest rates when short-term rates hit the zero lower bound
- **Forward guidance**: Communicating that rates would remain low for an extended period
- **Bank stress tests (SCAP, 2009)**: Forcing banks to raise capital, restoring confidence

### Controversies

- **"Greenspan put" continuation**: Critics argue Bernanke maintained excessively loose policy before the crisis, contributing to the housing bubble
- **Moral hazard**: Bailouts of large institutions (AIG, Bear Stearns via JPMorgan) set a precedent for "too big to fail"
- **QE side effects**: Wealth inequality (asset price inflation), distorted capital allocation, political independence concerns
- **The "Bernanke doctrine"**: His claim that the Fed had the tools to prevent deflation (2002 speech) was tested but recovery remained sluggish

## 反面论点与数据空白

- **金融加速器模型的局限**: BGG模型假设金融摩擦是周期波动的**放大器**,而非**源头**。但2008危机表明,金融部门自身可以产生冲击(投机→泡沫→崩溃)。Minsky的金融不稳定假说可能比BGG更适合解释内生金融危机,但教材未充分对比。
- **QE的有效性争议**: 实证研究显示QE对长期利率的影响有限且短暂;主要通过"信号渠道"(改变预期)而非"资产组合再平衡"。但Bernanke认为QE防止了第二次大萧条——这一反事实无法验证。
- **分配后果被忽略**: QE推高资产价格,使富人(持有股票/房产)受益,而穷人(依赖工资/储蓄)受损。教材对金融危机政策的分配维度讨论不足。
- **后危机监管的不足**: Dodd-Frank法案(2010)被认为过于复杂且部分被2018年放松。"太大而不能倒"问题未根本解决。
- **regional-bias**: 以美国Fed为中心;欧央行(Draghi "whatever it takes")、英国央行、日本央行(黑田东彦QQE)的危机应对差异巨大,教材未系统比较。
