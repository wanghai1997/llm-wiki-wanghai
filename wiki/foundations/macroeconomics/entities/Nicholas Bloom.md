# Nicholas Bloom

**Nicholas Bloom** (b. 1973), economist at Stanford University, is a leading empirical researcher on management practices, uncertainty, and productivity. He is best known for the **World Management Survey**, which demonstrated that differences in management quality explain a significant portion of cross-country and cross-firm productivity gaps.

## The Management-Productivity Link

In a series of papers with [[John Van Reenen]] and collaborators (beginning with Bloom and Van Reenen, 2007, *Quarterly Journal of Economics*), Bloom developed and applied a standardized survey instrument to measure management practices across thousands of firms in dozens of countries.

### Key Findings

1. **Management quality varies enormously**: Across firms within the same country and industry, management scores vary by as much as the difference between the 10th and 90th percentile firms
2. **Management explains TFP**: Differences in management practices account for roughly **one-quarter to one-third** of cross-country TFP differences
3. **US management is highest among surveyed countries**: In early waves (US, UK, France, Germany), US firms scored highest on average, with a significant gap to European counterparts
4. **Competition drives good management**: Firms in more competitive product markets tend to have better management practices, suggesting that market selection weeds out poorly managed firms
5. **Family-owned firms often underperform**: Family firms that pass management to heirs rather than professional managers tend to have lower management scores

### The Survey Instrument

The World Management Survey evaluates practices along multiple dimensions:
- **Operations**: Use of lean manufacturing, continuous improvement, adoption of modern techniques
- **Monitoring**: Collection and use of performance data, KPI tracking, performance dialogues
- **Targets**: Setting appropriate, challenging, and interconnected goals
- **Incentives**: Linking pay to performance, promoting high-performers, dealing with underperformers

### Limitations and Critiques

The survey methodology and sample have been criticized:
- **Sample bias**: Early waves focused on manufacturing in rich countries (US, UK, France, Germany), excluding East Asian manufacturing powerhouses (Japan's lean production, South Korean chaebol management, Chinese factory management) and most emerging markets
- **Western-centric definition of "good management"**: The survey instrument may implicitly favor Anglo-American management styles over alternative models (e.g., Japanese kaizen, German co-determination, Chinese state-enterprise coordination)
- **Correlation vs causation**: While the cross-sectional correlation is strong, identifying the causal effect of management practices on productivity requires addressing reverse causality and omitted variable bias

The textbook (Ch 9-2 Case Study) presents Bloom-Van Reenen's findings as evidence that "management practices explain a large fraction of productivity differences" without noting these sample limitations—a [regional-bias] in the empirical base.
