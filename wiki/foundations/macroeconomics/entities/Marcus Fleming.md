# Marcus Fleming

**John Marcus Fleming** (1911–1976), British economist at the International Monetary Fund (IMF), co-developed the [[Mundell-Fleming模型]] with Canadian economist [[Robert Mundell]]. His 1962 paper *"Domestic Financial Policies Under Fixed and Under Floating Exchange Rates"* (IMF Staff Papers) established the key results independently of Mundell's subsequent 1963 work.

## Core Contribution: The Mundell-Fleming Model (1962)

Fleming extended the [[IS-LM模型]] to the open economy under the assumption of **perfect capital mobility** and a **small open economy** (where the domestic interest rate equals the world interest rate). His analysis yielded the now-famous policy effectiveness results:

- **Floating exchange rates**: Monetary policy is highly effective (works through exchange rate changes and net exports); fiscal policy is ineffective (fully crowded out by exchange rate appreciation)
- **Fixed exchange rates**: Fiscal policy is highly effective (central bank accommodates to maintain the peg); monetary policy is ineffective (must be subordinated to exchange rate defense)

These results are the foundation of modern open-economy macroeconomics and the [[不可能三角]].

## Relationship to Mundell's Work

Fleming and Mundell developed essentially the same model independently and nearly simultaneously:
- Fleming (1962): IMF Staff Papers, focused on policy assignment under alternative exchange rate regimes
- Mundell (1963): Canadian Journal of Economics, added optimal currency areas and the policy trilemma

The model is jointly named because both contributions were necessary and complementary—Fleming established the core mechanics, while Mundell extended the framework to currency unions and the impossible trinity.

## Relationship to Mankiw's Framework

Mankiw 9e Ch 13 presents the Mundell-Fleming model as the natural extension of IS-LM to the open economy, with Fleming (1962) cited as the co-originator alongside Mundell (1963).
