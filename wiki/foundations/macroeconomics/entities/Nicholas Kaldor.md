# Nicholas Kaldor

**Nicholas Kaldor** (1908–1986), Hungarian-British economist at Cambridge University, was a leading figure in post-Keynesian economics and made foundational contributions to growth theory, distribution theory, and applied economics.

## Kaldor's Stylized Facts (1961)

In a 1961 paper *"Capital Accumulation and Economic Growth"*, Kaldor summarized the key empirical regularities of advanced capitalist economies that any growth theory should explain. These **"stylized facts"** became the empirical benchmark against which growth models are evaluated:

1. Per-capita output grows over time, and its growth rate does not tend to diminish
2. Physical capital per worker grows over time
3. The rate of return on capital is roughly stable
4. The capital-output ratio is roughly stable
5. The shares of labor and capital in national income are roughly stable
6. The growth rate of output per worker differs substantially across countries

The [[Solow增长模型]] with exogenous labor-augmenting technological progress successfully explains facts 1–5 through the concept of the **[[平衡增长路径]]**, where capital, output, and the effective labor force all grow at the same rate. Fact 6—the persistence of cross-country income differences—motivated the augmented Solow model ([[Mankiw-Romer-Weil模型]]) and endogenous growth theory.

## Post-Keynesian Growth Theory

Kaldor also developed an alternative growth framework—the **Cambridge growth model** (Kaldor 1957)—in which the savings rate varies by income class (workers vs capitalists) and the distribution of income adjusts to ensure that savings equal investment at full employment. This "post-Keynesian" approach stands in contrast to the neoclassical Solow model, where the capital-output ratio adjusts while distribution remains determined by marginal productivity.

Mankiw's textbook does not present the post-Keynesian alternative, framing growth theory exclusively through the neoclassical lens—a methodological choice that reflects the textbook's mainstream orientation. See [[宏观经济学foundations总览]] §3.2 on the "New Keynesian synthesis" bias.
