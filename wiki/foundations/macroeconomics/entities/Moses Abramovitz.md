# Moses Abramovitz

**Moses Abramovitz** (1912–2000), Stanford University economist, was a pioneer of modern productivity analysis and growth accounting.

## "A Measure of Our Ignorance"

In his 1956 paper *"Resource and Output Trends in the United States Since 1870"*, Abramovitz documented that only about 15% of US per-capita output growth between 1869 and 1953 could be explained by increases in measured capital and labor inputs. The remaining 85%—the unexplained residual—he famously characterized as **"a measure of our ignorance"** about the true sources of economic growth.

This residual, which [[Robert Solow]] simultaneously formalized in his 1957 growth accounting framework, is now conventionally termed **total factor productivity (TFP)** or the "Solow residual." Abramovitz's framing emphasizes the residual's epistemological status: it captures everything the model cannot measure, including:
- Technological progress
- Improvements in resource allocation
- Economies of scale
- Human capital formation (not yet separately measured in early studies)
- Measurement error in inputs

## Subsequent Research

Later work reduced the "ignorance" share by:
- Incorporating human capital ([[Mankiw-Romer-Weil模型]] 1992)
- Better quality-adjusted capital measures (computers, IT)
- Incorporating capacity utilization and reallocation dynamics

Yet even with these refinements, TFP remains a significant component of growth in most empirical decompositions, particularly for explaining cross-country income differences (see [[要素积累vs生产效率]]).
